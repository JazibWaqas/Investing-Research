# MEBL (Meezan Bank): underwriting card

Written 17 Oct 2026, before the 22 Oct Q3 result. Price Rs545.82 (close 16 Oct 2026, data/prices_raw/MEBL.csv). KMI All Share member (data/kmi_allshare_2026-06-05.csv). **Verdict: WATCH. This is a quality franchise at a full price. Buy at Rs490 or below.** Expected 12-month return at today's price is about 6%, which is below the 12-month T-bill (12.5%).

## The story

Meezan is Pakistan's largest Islamic bank and the best deposit franchise in the KMI universe. It earns a spread: Rs222.8bn return on financing and sukuk against Rs94.0bn paid to depositors in H1 2026, giving a net spread of Rs128.8bn (standalone, issuer filing). The funding is the moat. Current accounts are 49% of deposits and CASA is 91%, so when the policy rate rises, asset yields reprice faster than funding costs. Most of the Rs5.1tn balance sheet sits in government Ijarah sukuk (Rs2.90tn of investments against Rs1.59tn of financing), so earnings track the sukuk curve more than credit demand.

Why look now: SBP hiked 100bp in April (to 11.5%) and the 6M T-bill at 12.40% prices another hike. Q2 consolidated net spread jumped about 10% QoQ (Rs67.4bn against Rs61.4bn in Q1) as the hike fed through, and Q2 consolidated EPS was 14.23 against 12.31 in Q1. Q3 on 22 Oct should be the first full quarter at the higher rate. That is the bull story. The bear story is that the market already knows it: the stock is up 20% in a year (Rs454 to Rs546) while ROE has fallen from 56% (2023) to 33%.

**Relative P/E.** MEBL's fiscal-year P/E for 2021 to 2025 was 7.73, 3.96, 3.38, 4.23, 8.82. Against rough KSE-100 proxies for the same years, the relative ratio was 1.29, 0.97, 0.99, 1.09, 0.96, with a median about 0.99x (seed analysis; years not synchronised, unverified). Today it is 10.7x against a KSE-100 median of 8.43x on the 16 Oct screener, about 1.27x. Against the roughly 7x market forward P/E (unverified) it is nearer 1.4x. On its own history MEBL is expensive relative to the market.

**P/B against ROE.** Justified P/B = (ROE - g) / (CoE - g). With ROE 33%, g 10% and CoE 18% (T-bill 12.4% plus about 6% equity premium) I get 2.9x, which on 30 Jun book is Rs470 and on a 12-month-forward book of about Rs190 is Rs550. At g 11% and CoE 17% it is 3.7x. The answer moves Rs150 for a 1-point change in CoE, so P/B tells you only that today's price is roughly fair, not cheap. The orchestrator's Rs490 is the spot-book version of this; I agree with it as a buy level, not as the 12-month value.

Conclusion: not mispriced. The franchise deserves a premium, and it already has one.

## What kills it

1. **Easing cycle starts.** Trigger: 6M T-bill falls below the policy rate, or SBP makes a first cut. After the first cut in 2020 and 2024 Islamic banks lagged the market by 48 to 49 points over six months (tests/rate_cycle_episodes.md). Sell on that trigger without waiting for the result.
2. **Tax.** Trigger: FY28 Finance Bill (June 2027) raises the bank tax rate, the super tax, or reinstates an ADR-linked levy.
3. **Spread squeeze.** Trigger: consolidated quarterly EPS below Rs12 for two quarters, or CASA falling below 85%.
4. **Credit.** Trigger: NPL ratio above 3% or quarterly provisions above Rs5bn (Q2 was Rs2.15bn), most likely through the oil shock.
5. **Market de-rating.** Trigger: KSE-100 forward P/E back toward 5x. MEBL's beta is 0.58, but at 1.3x relative P/E it has room to fall.
6. **Dividend cut.** Trigger: an interim below Rs7.

## Red team (17 Oct 2026)

Checked against data/prices_raw/MEBL.csv, data/panel_close_adj.parquet, data/fundamentals/quarterly_eps.csv, data/announcements.parquet (54 MEBL rows since Oct 2025, titles only; no PDFs opened) and tests/. Today 17 Oct 2026.

**Clean checks.**
- Share count and EPS basis are fine. The four quarters sum to 50.90 consolidated, and Q2 checks against Rs25.73bn / 1.807bn.
- Liquidity is not a problem. The 60-day median traded value is Rs325m (13th of the liquid panel) and the 90-day median is Rs427m. Rs12k is trivial.
- 2026 announcements for MEBL contain no suspension, unusual-movement letter, non-compliance notice or auditor change. The titles also show no pledge. I did not read the PDFs behind the 9 Feb "Disclosure of Material Information", the 15 Jul cluster of six director/executive interest disclosures (a few days after the 9 Jul ESOS allotment, so probably option exercises), or the 17 Aug director cessation (Mr. Tariq Mahmood Pasha) and 8 Oct "change of directors" notices. Treat them as unread, not clean.
- A new CEO was appointed on 30 Dec 2025. A CEO change, two board changes and an undisclosed material-information notice inside ten months is a governance footnote the card omits.

**Findings that damage the case.**

1. **The Q2 jump is mostly associates, not the hike, and the catalyst is already in the price.**
   - Q1 to Q2 consolidated EPS rose 1.92 (12.31 to 14.23). Share of associates swung from -Rs1.2bn to +Rs1.48bn, which is about Rs1.5 per share. That is roughly 78% of the QoQ rise.
   - H1 associates were only +0.26bn, so Q2 core EPS is nearer 13.5.
   - The 28 Apr hike was already in two of Q2's three months, so Q3 is not a clean "first full quarter" step-up. A Q3 print of about 13.5 to 14 is a non-event, and the 7 post-result windows are median -0.4%, 43% up.
   - The price has priced the hike. Since 30 Apr it is up 11% (492 to 546), and it peaked at 599.61 on 11 Aug. Over 6 months MEBL is +13% against a liquid-universe median of -6% (BIPL -14%, BAHL -13%, HBL -7%, FABL -1%, UBL +1%). It is the sector's only winner, which is a crowded relative trade going into a rate-cut tail risk.
   - EPS trend: CY24 was 57.3, CY25 50.5, TTM 50.9. The base case needs CY28 EPS 60 and the bull needs 65, so both rest on beating the 2024 peak.
2. **Base case multiple contradicts the card's own relative P/E.**
   - The card says MEBL is at 1.27 to 1.4x the market against a 0.99x history. Yet base exits at 9.3x on forward EPS 60 against a 7x market (1.33x), which assumes the premium never mean-reverts.
   - At 1.1x of 7x (7.7x) on EPS 60, value is Rs462. At the card's own justified P/B (2.9x, Rs470 on spot book) the stock is above fair.
   - The base sits above both of the card's own cross-checks.
3. **The probabilities are too tight.**
   - The scenarios imply a 12-month standard deviation of about 16% (Rs425/560/680, weights .25/.55/.20). MEBL's realised annual volatility is 30%. Adjusted 12-month forward returns, which are rerating-contaminated per lesson 9, put the 5th percentile at -19%. The sample is mostly the 2023-25 rerating, so it cannot validate any tail.
   - Bear at 25% is probably fine on frequency, but its size is soft. tests/rate_cycle_episodes.md gives Islamic-bank 120-day excess of -38.2% and -32.5% after cuts (+4.0% in the third), mean -22.2%, 6th percentile of the placebo. A -22% bear (Rs425) is the mild end of that, not the middle.
   - A cut is not a 25% event on the card's own evidence. CPI is 10.3%, SPI is 11.4% and T-bills price a hike. A cut inside 12 months is less likely, so the bear is mostly tax and de-rating, and it needs a name.
