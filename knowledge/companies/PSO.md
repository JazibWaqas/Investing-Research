# PSO (Pakistan State Oil): underwriting card

Written 17 Oct 2026. Price Rs368.04 (16 Oct close, cum-dividend). KMI All Share member. Verdict: **watch, do not buy at this price.** Confidence medium.

## The story

PSO is Pakistan's largest fuel marketer, with about half of the white-oil market (639kt of September's 1.35mt industry sales). It earns a government-fixed margin of Rs7.87 a litre on petrol and diesel, about 2% of the pump price, plus whatever inventory gains or losses crude moves give it. It also owns a majority of Pakistan Refinery (PRL, stake about 63.6%, unverified), so its consolidated profit carries refining margins.

Three things make it interesting now. An ECC-approved Rs1.22 a litre margin increase (to about Rs9.09) has been waiting since August for a notification. Brent spot rose from $70.5 on 30 Jun to $115.9 on 30 Sep (FRED), so Q1FY27 should show an inventory gain. And the stock is 22% down on the year at 0.62x book. The catch is that the same three facts make it a political target. A windfall recovery of inventory gains (Section 99D, Rs72bn proposed in May) is still on the table, and the government has tried to tie the margin increase to a digitisation programme.

**What is one-off in the Rs54.29.** The quarters were Rs20 (Q1, sector note), Rs11.4 (Q2), Rs52.6 (Q3, war inventory gain) and minus Rs29.6 (Q4, the April diesel cut). The inventory gain and loss roughly cancel. The bigger distortion is the refinery. Consolidated less standalone is about Rs22 a share, roughly PSO's share of PRL's FY26 EPS of Rs25.05, earned on crisis crack spreads that will not last. Standalone OMC earnings were Rs32. Normal OMC earnings at today's margin are Rs35 to 40, and a normal PRL contribution is perhaps Rs10 to 12. **Normalised consolidated EPS is about Rs50**, so the stock trades at 7.4x normalised. Notification of the margin increase adds Rs8 to 11.

**Relative P/E.** On StockAnalysis fiscal-year-end ratios against the median of about 60 covered PSX names: FY23 1.20x, FY24 0.68x, FY25 0.98x, FY26 0.68x, an average of 0.89x. Today it is 0.84x against the KSE100 constituent median P/E of 8.0 (screener, 16 Oct). That is normal for PSO. It is not a discount. (The FY22 figure is distorted and excluded. The market median is a proxy, not the official index P/E.)

**P/B vs ROE.** With a normalised ROE of 8 to 10%, a 6-month T-bill at 12.4% and a cost of equity of about 17%, justified P/B is 0.5 to 0.6x. At 0.62x the stock is priced for about what it earns. It looks cheap because the book is mostly receivables from SNGPL, GENCOs and PIA, funded by short-term debt. Those receivables earn nothing until the government pays.

Conclusion: PSO is not mispriced on today's numbers. The upside is a policy option (margin notification, circular-debt settlement, rate cuts) that the market will not pay for in advance.

## How much it can move (12 months, ex-dividend values)

- **Bear Rs270, probability 25%.** A windfall recovery is enacted, or crude reverses and books another Q4FY26-style inventory loss. The margin stays unnotified, a PRL rights issue calls for PSO cash, and rates go up. Method: 0.45x book of Rs600, about the FY24 to 25 low-ROE multiple. This is below the 52-week low of Rs320 and the war low of Rs329.
- **Base Rs380, probability 55%.** Q1 prints a gain that adds about Rs30 to 40 to book. The margin is notified with about 50% odds. Receivables stay stuck. Method: 0.6x on a book of about Rs630, cross-checked at 7.2x normalised EPS of Rs50 plus half the margin value.
- **Bull Rs500, probability 20%.** The margin is notified, the circular-debt plan pays down receivables and rates are cut. Method: normalised EPS of Rs60 at 8.3x, relative P/E 1.0 to the market, about 0.8x book. Broker targets of Rs605 to 727 (StockAnalysis, AlphaSpread, TradingView, undated) are unverified and look stale.

Weighted value is Rs376.5. With the Rs8.50 net dividend, **expected 12-month return is +4.6%**, below the 6-month T-bill net of tax, with a 25% chance of a 24% loss.

## What kills it

1. Section 99D or another windfall recovery is notified (Gazette or Finance Division SRO). Exit.
2. Q1FY27 trade debts rise above Rs450bn, or short-term borrowing above Rs420bn. Receivables are worsening.
3. The margin hike is formally tied to digitisation or withdrawn, with no notification by the H1 results in February 2027.
4. Brent falls more than 25% within a quarter. That repeats the Q4FY26 inventory loss.
5. An MPC hike on 26 Oct or 14 Dec.
6. PRL rights issue terms that call for more than Rs10bn from PSO.
