# NETSOL Technologies Ltd (NETSOL): underwriting card

As of 17 Oct 2026. Price Rs122.66 (16 Oct close). KMI All Share: yes. Verdict: **watch, do not buy at Rs122**. Add below Rs105, or after a clean Q1 FY27 print. Confidence: medium.

## The story

NETSOL Pakistan is the Lahore delivery arm of NASDAQ-listed NetSol Technologies Inc (NTWK), which owns about 70% of it. It sells leasing and auto-finance software (NFS Ascent, Transcend) to auto captives and banks. Its revenue is in USD and EUR and its costs are in rupees. Tax is a final levy on export proceeds, about 10% of pre-tax profit in FY26.

FY26 (June year end, consolidated) was its best year ever. Revenue was Rs12.51bn (+26%), operating profit Rs2.83bn (+62%), net profit Rs2.35bn and EPS **Rs27.25** (FY25: 15.97). On that EPS the stock is 4.5x trailing, has net cash of about a third of its market cap and trades at 0.83x book. There is also a structural angle. In its 28 Sep 2026 FY26 release, the parent said it is "evaluating structural options that may reduce the economic impact of minority interests over time". On the Q3 call it called buying out the remaining 30% a "top priority" that needs financing (secondary report, unverified).

**The one-off.** In Q3 FY26 (Jan to Mar 2026) the parent recognised a $4.66m one-time Transcend licence on a four-year, $50m extension with a Mercedes-Benz captive. If all of it had landed in Pakistan, it would be about Rs1.3bn, or Rs15 of EPS: the orchestrator's "half of EPS". The Pakistan books do not show that much. Q3 consolidated gross margin was 58.5%, against 48.7% in Q2 and 46.8% in Q4. Treating the excess as a 100%-margin licence on top of a 48% underlying margin gives about Rs735m of revenue, or Rs660m after levies: **Rs7.7 per share**. A narrower read gives about Rs3.5: Q3 net profit was Rs950m, against an average of Rs663m for Q2 and Q4. I use Rs7.7, which makes normalised EPS Rs19.6. The trailing 4.6x headline is really about 6.3x.

**Quality problems the headline hides.** Trade debts are Rs6.94bn, about 200 days of revenue, and expected-credit-loss charges were Rs330m in FY25 and Rs78m in FY26. The year-end cash is also flattered. Contract liabilities (customer advances) rose from Rs153m to Rs1.79bn. Operating cash flow of Rs2.74bn includes Rs1.64bn of those advances, so underlying cash conversion is about 50% of profit. Net cash excluding the advances is about Rs22/share. Payout is 5.5% (a Rs1.50 final; nothing was paid in FY26 for FY25). The CFO changed on 29 Sep 2026.

**Relative P/E.** The repo has no KSE100 P/E series. NETSOL's fiscal-year-end P/Es were 10.3x (2022), 6.3x (2023), 9.9x (2024), 8.2x (2025) and 4.9x (2026) (seed `sa_ratios`). The market sat at about 3.5 to 4x in 2022-23 (lesson 9) and is quoted at 7.9x now (Nukta, date unclear). On those figures NETSOL has gone from about 1.5 to 2.5x the market multiple to about 0.8x on normalised EPS. Part of that is real: the stock is now cheap relative to the market. Part of it is the market waking up to the company's working capital and its minority structure (unverified).

**Justified P/B check (lesson 6).** With ROE of 14%, growth of 5% and cost of equity of 18% (T-bill 12.4% plus a 6% equity premium), justified P/B is (14−5)/(18−5) = 0.69x, or Rs102. At the reported 19.5% ROE it is 1.12x, or Rs165. The market price sits between the two. This is cheap, not a mispricing.

Probability-weighted value is Rs126.6. With the Rs1.275 dividend, **expected 12-month return is about +4.3%**, below the 12.4% six-month T-bill. The headline cheapness is mostly the one-off. A buyout is unlikely within 12 months: the 10-K says about $26.5m of the $27.1m group cash sits in foreign subsidiaries, which leaves the US parent about $0.6m. A 30% stake costs about Rs4bn ($14m). The more plausible route is another NETSOL PK buyback. The last one, in FY25, bought 2.69m shares for Rs420m, about Rs156/share.

## What kills it

1. **Q1 FY27 EPS below Rs3.** That would mean the Q2 and Q4 run-rate of Rs7.4 to 7.8 was project timing, not trend.
2. **Trade debts above Rs7.5bn**, or ECL charges above Rs150m a year, or operating cash flow excluding contract liabilities turning negative.
3. **A parent "structural action" that moves contracts or IP to wholly owned entities**, or a related-party notice that shifts revenue away from NETSOL PK. This is the minority's worst case. In 2010-11 the SECP blocked a related-party restructuring. It is the regulator to watch.
4. **USD/PKR below about 270**, which squeezes the rupee cost base.
5. **Loss or renegotiation of the Mercedes captive contract** or another top-five customer. NTWK's Asia-Pacific segment is 69% of group revenue.
6. **Tech sector relative momentum stays bottom-three** (lesson 12).
