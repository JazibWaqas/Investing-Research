# FABL (Faysal Bank): quality of management and sponsor

Written 18 Oct 2026. Sources: FY2021 to FY2025 annual reports (AR) and the 2024, 2025 and 2026 corporate briefing decks (CBS), downloaded from PSX into `data/raw/filings/quality/FABL/` and never executed. Short PSX notices are mostly scanned images; those are marked unverified.

## People and record

- **CEO Yousaf Hussain.** CEO since 2017, at the bank since 2008, ex-ABN AMRO (AR 2025, p.93). Reappointed for three years from 15 Aug 2026 (board 12 May 2026; Profit, 15 May 2026). He led the conversion to a fully Islamic bank, completed 1 Jan 2023 (AR 2022). His shareholding: unverified.
- **Sponsor.** Ithmaar Bank B.S.C. holds 66.78%, ultimately Dar Al-Maal Al-Islami Trust; no other holder is above 10% (AR 2025, 31 Dec 2025). Chair Mian Muhammad Younis (since at least the FY2023 report, 14 Feb 2024; Farooq Rahmatullah Khan chaired in FY2021). Vice chair Ahmed Bucheery was Ithmaar's CEO until Sep 2022; Juma Abul is DMIT group CEO.
- **CFO** changed from Syed Majid Ali (CBS 14 Apr 2025) to Tanveer Khatri (notice 12 Dec 2025, PSX 267182; CBS 3 Apr 2026).
- No FABL rows in `data/insider_trades.csv`.

Operational promises were kept, with one explained miss. Profit promises held while rates rose, then lost their numbers. A 2026 to 2030 plan exists but no financial targets were published (AR 2025, p.39).

## Big capital decisions

- **Islamic conversion** (2018 to 2023 plan). PAT Rs8.2bn (2021) to Rs23.0bn (2024); ROE 12.9% to 25.0% to 19.4% (2025). The policy rate rose from 7% to 22% meanwhile, so the conversion's own return cannot be isolated. Price to book rose from 0.53x to 1.22x (CBS 2026).
- **Branches.** 576 (2020) to 900, with 133 in 2024. Cost-to-income went from 49% to 51%. Deposits hit Rs1.43tn in 2025 (+37%) after an ADR tax capped 2024 (CBS 2025).
- **Small items.** Exchange company (Rs1.0bn 2024, Rs0.2bn Jan 2026); Rs7bn Tier II sukuk (25 May 2026) because CAR is about 14% (PACRA, 2026). No value-destroying acquisition is visible.

## Minorities

- **No equity raised.** Shares stayed 1,516m to 1,518m, 2021 to 2026 (`quarterly_eps.csv`). The Jun 2026 SECP application (up to 107m shares, only on a regulator-declared non-viability event) comes from the existing card; terms unverified.
- **Dividends per share:** 2021 about Rs1.5 (payout 9%); 2022 Rs7.0, including a Rs5.5 interim in Nov 2022, about 94% of EPS; 2023 Rs4.0; 2024 Rs7.0; 2025 Rs6.5 (about 45%). The 2022 AGM-EOGM record mentions Shariah cleansing of retained earnings; the cause of the large payout is unconfirmed.
- **Related parties.** The parent column in note 44 (AR 2025) is near zero, so no leakage to Ithmaar is visible.
- **Control sale.** Ithmaar holders approved selling 75% of its Faysal stake (about 50% of Faysal) to GFH in May 2024; talks ended 16 Apr 2025 with the door left open (Business Recorder; Pak Banker). Minority exit terms were never stated. Maple Leaf Cement bought Faysal shares on market in 2025 and got CCP approval on 12 Mar 2026 to buy more; stake size unverified.

## Disclosure

Results come about 7 weeks after year end and 3 to 4 weeks after quarters, with a yearly briefing and posted deck. The AR is detailed and carries the promise table. KPMG replaced A.F. Ferguson after FY2021 (routine). Negatives: the 6 Aug 2026 half-year result was revoked the same day over a cash-flow typo (PSX 280814, 280856; reason from the existing card, notice unread); no earnings guidance in any CBS; image-only notices.

## Growth engine and what breaks it

Book grows about 9% a year (about 17% ROE with 55% retained). With the balance sheet growing about 14% a year (CASA, remittance share 4.2% to 6.1%, CBSME +58% in 2025), EPS roughly 1.5x in five years. Doubling needs about 15% a year, so ROE would have to stay near 19%. That is not the base case. It breaks on rate cuts (net profit return already -13% in 2025), a tax rate near 51%, the Islamic minimum-deposit-rate formula, thin capital, and a sponsor sale at an unknown price.

## Red flags

1. Sponsor wants out; failed sale; unknown buyer accumulating.
2. Earnings are rate-driven; 2025 leaned on Rs7bn more capital gains and reversals.
3. CASA mix fell in 2023 and 2025.
4. Revoked results (Aug 2026); CFO and chair turnover.
5. Promises lost their numbers after 2023.

## Scores (0 to 5)

- **Promises versus delivery: 4.** Every quantified promise was met except 2023 branches; later ones are unfalsifiable.
- **Capital allocation: 3.** Big conversion and branch build paid off, but returns are inseparable from the rate cycle and ROE sits near cost of equity.
- **Minority treatment: 3.** No dilution, steady payouts, clean related-party flows; but an exit sale with no stated minority terms.
- **Disclosure: 4.** Full reports and briefings with a promise table, marked down for the revocation and thin guidance.

**Overall 14 of 20.**
