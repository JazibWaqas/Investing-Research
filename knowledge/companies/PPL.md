# PPL (Pakistan Petroleum Ltd): underwriting card

As of 2026-10-17. Price is the 16 Oct 2026 close. KMI All Share member (data/kmi_allshare_2026-06-05.csv). Verdict: **watch, not buy at Rs219**. Start a position at Rs185 or lower after the ex-date, or when the CCoE approves the gas debt plan and the IMF agrees to it.

## The story

PPL is Pakistan's second-largest state E&P. It sells gas (Sui, Kandhkot, Adhi, Gambat South, Nashpa) at OGRA-set wellhead prices and oil and condensate (TAL, Nashpa) at prices linked to Arab Light. It books good profits but collects only part of them. Trade receivables from the Sui companies are Rs623bn to Rs630bn, about Rs230 per share, more than the share price. The FY26 recovery ratio was 86% in Q4 (TechJuice). So PPL is a claim on oil, plus an option on the government settling the gas circular debt.

Why it matters now: Brent is about USD 106 with Hormuz closed, OGRA raised wellhead gas prices 8 to 10% for Jul to Dec 2026, and the Petroleum Division's Rs1.495tr debt plan would send about Rs229bn of extra dividends from PPL to the state. Payouts have already jumped, from Rs2 a share in FY22 to Rs12 in FY26.

**The one-off.** FY26 pre-tax profit was Rs135.9bn and tax Rs38.2bn, an effective rate of 28.1%. FY25's rate was 34.3%. Q4 FY26 alone was taxed at about 10.5%, against about 35% in each of the first three quarters. TechJuice links this to the super tax reversal. At FY25's rate, FY26 profit would have been Rs89.3bn, or EPS 32.8. Lower exploration write-offs (Rs12.1bn against 18.5bn) also helped, and they swing from year to year. A Rs3bn dry hole (Ishraq-X-1) is already expected in 1QFY27.

**Forward.** Topline puts 1QFY27 at EPS 10.81, +46% YoY, on Arab Light about USD 90 (Bloom Pakistan, 6 Oct 2026). Stockanalysis' forward P/E of 5.49 implies about Rs40 for FY27. On that, PPL is 5.5x, or about 4.5x after net cash. That headline is the cheap case.

**Relative P/E.** This is the sceptic's point. Comparing PPL's trailing P/E with the median of June-year-end names in seed/data/fundamentals/sa_ratios.csv gives 0.52x (Jun 2022), 0.35x (2023), 0.44x (2024), 0.47x (2025), 0.73x (Jun 2026) and 0.78x now. I use that median as a stand-in for the KSE-100. I could not get the KSE-100's own aggregate P/E history (unverified). On a regime-aware basis, PPL is at the top of its five-year range, not the bottom. The rerating from about 0.45x already priced in better collections and the jump to Rs12 of dividends. A 6x P/E is cheap in absolute terms. It is not cheap against PPL's own history relative to the market.

**Book.** A P/B of 0.77 looks like a discount, but about 80% of book is receivables. Excluding them, book is about Rs54 a share. The market already prices the receivables at roughly half value. The equity case therefore rests on the debt plan.

**Same story as OGDC?** Mostly. OGDC trades at 5.4x on a 16.9% ROE and 0.86x book, and its Rs122 a share of receivables is a smaller share of its price. PPL is the more leveraged bet on a debt deal, for better and for worse.

## How much it can move (12 months, ex-dividend values)

- **Bear, Rs155, p = 0.30.** Hormuz reopens and Brent falls to USD 65 to 70, so EPS goes back to about Rs34 (FY25 was 33 on USD 61 realised). The IMF blocks the plan or pushes the write-off of inter-corporate debt and LPS it asked for on 2 Oct. The relative P/E returns to 0.55x on a market at about 8, which is 4.5x. The April 2025 low was Rs148.
- **Base, Rs222, p = 0.50.** Oil eases to the high 70s, and EPS a year forward is about Rs35. The plan stays in limbo and collections run near 85 to 90%. The relative P/E holds at about 0.78x on a market at 8, which is 6.3x.
- **Bull, Rs295, p = 0.20.** The CCoE approves the plan with IMF concurrence and the first extra payouts arrive. Rs229bn to a roughly 70% state holder implies about Rs120 a share gross over several years (my inference; stake unverified). The rating moves to 1.0x book (about Rs300) or 7.5x Rs40. The Jan 2026 high was Rs281.

Probability-weighted value: Rs216.5. Adding Rs10.20 of net dividends against Rs218.87 gives an **expected 12-month return of about +3.6%**. That is below the 11.5% policy rate and T-bills. Big special dividends do not create value on the ex-date, since the price falls by the payout. The gain comes only from the receivables being re-marked from about 50 paisa to the rupee.

## What kills it

1. **IMF write-off.** An IMF staff report or a Finance Ministry statement that PPL's receivables or LPS will be written off or netted. Trigger: any dated document saying so. Book could fall by tens of rupees a share.
2. **Oil.** Brent below USD 75 for a month on a Hormuz reopening. Trigger: Hormuz reopening confirmed by UKMTO or CENTCOM.
3. **Collections.** Quarterly trade receivables up more than Rs15bn QoQ, or a recovery ratio below 80%. Trigger: the 1Q and 2Q results.
4. **Payout cut.** A 1QFY27 interim below Rs2. Trigger: the board notice around 29 Oct.
5. **Gas curtailment and RLNG surplus** cutting Sui and Kandhkot offtake. Trigger: Petroleum Division curtailment notices.
