# ATRL (Attock Refinery): underwriting card

Written 17 Oct 2026. Price Rs1,219.10 (16 Oct close, already ex the Rs15 final, ex-date 12 Oct). KMI All Share and KMI30 member. Verdict: **watch, do not chase. Buy only near Rs1,000.** Confidence medium.

## The story

ATRL refines mostly local northern crude at Morgah (main unit 32,400 bpd). It earns the diesel and petrol crack, interest on a large cash pile, and its share of listed associates NRL and APL. Consolidated FY26 profit was Rs26.06bn (EPS Rs244.45) against Rs8.95bn (Rs83.93) in FY25; standalone EPS was Rs207.32.

Why now: Hormuz pushed diesel cracks to $50 to 60, ATRL signed the upgrade agreement with ISGS on 24 Sep 2026 (PSX doc 283339), and Topline expects Q1FY27 EPS of Rs118 to 138, half of last year in one quarter. The board meets 4pm Monday 19 Oct; results reach PSX Tuesday 20 Oct (doc 285290). The catch: the market has seen all of it. The stock is up 43% since 30 June (Rs850.67), 77% in a year, and closed at its 52-week high.

**What is one-off in the Rs244.** Q3 (Rs118.79) was the war quarter: crack spike plus inventory gains. Q4 absorbed a main-unit shutdown from 22 Apr to about 4 May (road closures, docs 274758, 276605); February saw a heavy-unit shutdown on unlifted petrol (doc 269743). Interest income of about Rs9.7bn pre-tax (Rs55/share after tax) recurs but shrinks as upgrade capex spends the cash. Associates added Rs4.9bn, net of a Rs0.35bn NRL impairment.

**Normalised EPS of about Rs185** is my build: interest Rs55 to 60, refining at mid-cycle Rs80 to 90 (FY25's core refining earned about Rs34 after tax and FY26's about Rs150), and associates Rs40. That puts the stock at **6.6x normalised**.

**Sum of parts** looks like a bargain: Rs811 net cash plus Rs262 of listed stakes leaves Rs146 a share for a refinery that earned about Rs146 a share from pure refining in FY26. But the cash is not shareholders' money in practice. Topline puts the upgrade at $500 to 600m (Rs150 to 180bn), more than the whole cash balance. Payout was 7% (Rs17.50 on Rs244), and a special reserve has historically restricted distribution of refinery profits (unverified for FY26). The market discounts trapped cash, rightly.

**Relative P/E** (ATRL fiscal-year-end P/E over the sa_ratios median): FY22 0.23, FY23 0.13, FY24 0.25, FY25 0.74, FY26 0.40, average 0.35. Today 0.59 on trailing EPS against the KSE100 constituent median of 8.43 (screener, 16 Oct), about 0.41 if Q1 lands at Rs128, and 0.78 on normalised EPS. ATRL always trades at a deep relative discount; today it is not cheap against that history.

Conclusion: not mispriced. The upside is a bet that cracks stay high for a year and the diesel cap is left alone, not a valuation gap.

## How much it can move (12 months, ex-dividend values)

- **Bear Rs850, probability 30%.** The cap is cut to $30, or a Hormuz deal sends crude and cracks down together. The quarter after Q1 then shows inventory losses, and the stock gives back the policy rally. Method: the 30 June 2026 price, before the policy fast-track, is about 0.45x a June 2027 book of about Rs1,950. That matches the FY25 multiple of 0.47x.
- **Base Rs1,300, probability 50%.** Q1 is roughly in line, the cap is extended at $41.89, and cracks fade through H2. FY27 EPS is about Rs280 to 300. Method: 7x normalised EPS of Rs185, a relative P/E of about 0.8. Cross-check: about 0.65x June 2027 book.
- **Bull Rs1,650, probability 20%.** Cracks stay above $45 all year, there is no cap cut, and upgrade incentives accrue as income. FY27 EPS is about Rs380. Method: 0.8x a June 2027 book of about Rs2,060. This matches Topline's Rs1,570 (Sep 2027) and the StockAnalysis consensus of Rs1,610 (3 analysts).

Weighted value is Rs1,235. With Rs14.875 of net dividends, **expected 12-month return is +2.5%**. That is below a T-bill, with a 30% chance of losing 29%.

## What kills it

1. A $30 HSD crack cap or a landed-cost formula is notified (Petroleum Division or OGRA price notice).
2. Q1FY27 EPS comes in below Rs90, or management guides GRM below $15 a barrel for Q2.
3. Brent falls more than 25% in a quarter (a Hormuz settlement). That means inventory losses.
4. Upgrade capex is announced above $700m, or the financing plan needs debt or a rights issue, or the dividend is cut.
5. Utilisation falls below 60% in the AHL monthly upliftment data. Shutdowns in February and April 2026 show that this happens.
6. Deemed duty goes to zero on 15 Nov with no incentive notified to replace it.

## Red team (17 Oct 2026)

**Verdict: WEAKENED. Corrected expected 12-month return +0.5%, not +2.5%, and the honest range is about -3% to +10%. "Watch, do not chase" stands.**

**Checks that passed.**
- Share count and EPS basis: the card's Rs244.45 x 106.62m shares gives Rs26.06bn, which ties. Consolidated is the right basis, but see objection 2.
- Liquidity: the median traded value over the last 60 days is Rs562m a day (lowest day Rs99m), so Rs12k is trivial.
- Suspension or unusual-movement letters: none in announcements.parquet for ATRL in 2026. The 2026 filings are routine: results, shutdowns, board meetings, a Shariah disclosure and a change of alternate director (2 Mar).
- Auditor change and sponsor pledges: none found.
- Insider filings: none since 6 Nov 2025, and the direction field is "unknown" for all of them.
- Related party: the hospital sale to the chairman is Rs305m, about Rs3 a share, and immaterial.
- Dividends: Rs17.50 on Rs244 EPS is a 7% payout against Rs116bn of cash, so the problem is retention for the upgrade, not ability to pay. The Rs14.875 is only 1.2% of the price either way.
- Not verifiable here: the "special reserve" restriction. The FY26 filing PDFs (281722, 285290, 283339) are image-only and have no text layer, so this needs a manual read.

**Objection 1: the catalyst is largely priced, and the repo's own record of this stock says good prints get sold.**
- ATRL is up 12.7% in 21 days, 31.8% in 63 days and 46% in 126 days (adjusted prices). An equal-weight liquid-stock basket is -6.6% over 63 days and +5.4% over 126 days.
- The sector is split: NRL is +55% over 63 days, while OGDC, PPL and PSO are flat. This is a refiner-specific rally.
- Topline's Rs118-138 Q1 range is public, so a print at Rs128 is the base case, not a surprise.
- tests/refiner_steel_retrospective.md shows that after the record 29 Apr print (Rs106 EPS), the four refiners lost 16 to 22 points relative to the market over ten weeks.
- Volume is not a blow-off (last day 316k shares against a 60-day median of 521k), so I would not call it a crowded trade.

**Objection 2: normalised EPS of Rs185 is overstated, which flatters "6.6x" and the Rs1,300 base.**
- Rs55-60 of the Rs185 is interest on cash that the card itself says goes to a $500-600m upgrade. Cash falls, so that income falls.
- The Rs40 of associate income comes from NRL and APL. NRL is +94% over six months, so its FY26 earnings come from the same crack windfall and are not mid-cycle.
- The card also capitalises that cash and those stakes at 7x in EPS while valuing them at face in the sum of parts. It is one or the other.
- With interest at Rs35 and associates at Rs25, normalised EPS is about Rs150-160, or 7.6-8.1x today. Base Rs1,300 becomes about Rs1,250 if I blend the earnings and book methods.

**Objection 3: the probabilities are judgment, not base rates, and they point in both directions.**
- I tested point-in-time first signals (at least 120 days apart) of a 52-week high plus a 63-day gain above 25%, liquid names, 2018-2026.
- Over 60 days, n=442, median +0.3%, win 52%. Random liquid days: median -0.5%, win 49%.
- Over 250 days, n=393, median +17.9%, win 63%. Random days: median +0.8%, win 51%. The share losing more than 29% was 14.5% against the card's 30%, and the share gaining more than 37% was 39% against the card's 20%.
- Those 250-day numbers are clustered. Signals in 2020, 2023 and 2024 had positive medians (+33%, +48%, +44%), and signals in 2018 and 2021 had medians of -32% and -38%.
- ATRL's own five prior signals with a 12-month result gave +58%, -20%, +69%, +36% and +47% (the Jan and Aug 2026 signals have no 12-month result yet).
- Lesson 9 says the 2023-2025 rerating flatters the high-sample years. Treat this as a wide band, not a refutation of the bear weight. The card's 30/50/20 split has no test behind it.
