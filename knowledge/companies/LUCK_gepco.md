# LUCK and GEPCO: how big is the cheque (19 Oct 2026)

**Conclusion.** No sale price or reserve price for GEPCO has been published (searched Privatisation Commission, Dawn, Tribune, ProPakistani, Mettis, Cemnet; not found). My estimate of the whole consortium's cheque for 51% is Rs35 to 60bn, central Rs50bn. An even four-way split puts about Rs12.5bn on Lucky, which is 23% of FY26 operating cash flow and 16% of standalone net cash. That is a manageable test of allocation, not a thesis breaker. The breaker is a Lucky share above about Rs35bn. I put the consortium's chance of winning GEPCO at about 15%, below the 25% guessed.

## Existing commitments
- **PIA:** the consortium (same four partners) lost at Rs134bn against Rs135bn for 75% on 23 Dec 2025. Lucky's board had approved the bid on 18 Dec (doc 267525) but no per-partner split or amount was ever disclosed (not found). Nothing is committed now. The winner had to put 92.5% of the price back into the airline and at least Rs80bn over five years (reports; terms differ by source), a reminder that DISCO buyers may face the same post-deal injection, which is unquantified for GEPCO.
- **Lucky Electric:** Rs29.9bn invested at cost, now paying Rs12bn a year. No new capex found.
- **Lucky Motor (KIA, GAC):** Rs12.9bn at cost; no capex plan found.
- **Congo:** 1.6 Mtpa line, 50% joint venture, due mid FY28; cost in US dollars not found. National Resources: up to Rs1.2bn more. Together these sit inside the roughly Rs21bn a year of capex.
- **Other DISCOs:** the same consortium is also prequalified for FESCO (document 281942) and the IESCO groups. A cheque for GEPCO is not the only one; the real exposure is the sum, which the filings do not give.

## Timeline and probability
Government target: FESCO January 2027, IESCO March 2027, GEPCO February 2027 (IMF plan, reported). Process: expressions of interest closed 21 Aug; the consortium was shortlisted for due diligence on 7 Oct; binding bids follow. No bid date was found. Earlier schedules slipped (EOI deadlines were extended; the PIA took from July prequalification to December bidding, and closing in spring), so a February 2027 sale is likely to slip.

Win probability: 11 expressions of interest, including Engro Energy, K-Electric, Sapphire, three Turkish firms and a Saudi contractor; the count that qualified is not published. A naive share is 1 in 8 to 10. The group has the most cash of any named bidder and came within Rs1bn at PIA, but it also bids for FESCO and may stretch itself, and the board has said it will commit only if "commercially viable". My estimate is 15% (range 10 to 25%). The chance that Lucky's board approves a cheque above Rs20bn by March 2027 is lower, about 10%.

## Is the HUBC distinction real?
Yes, on the numbers, for the dividend. HUBC's exit trigger was an equity commitment above about Rs20bn against a dividend that was the whole thesis; Hub Power Holdings holds only Rs29.8bn of cash, so Rs20bn is two thirds of it. Lucky's dividend is Rs7.3bn against Rs15.8bn of subsidiary dividends; a Rs12.5bn cheque is 1.7 years of dividend but is not needed from it. Even Rs44bn leaves Rs53bn of standalone net cash.

What it does cost: forgone interest. At about 11% pre-tax and 20% tax, each Rs10bn removes about Rs0.60 a share, or 1% of EPS of Rs60.78. Rs12.5bn costs 1.2%; Rs38bn costs 3.7%. GEPCO's profit share at central price (13.7bn x 51% x 25% = Rs1.75bn) is about 14% on cost, against about 9% after tax on cash. That is roughly equal to cash returns, with tariff and political risk and no growth. So the cheque tests allocation quality and, at the central price, it passes only narrowly.

## What size breaks the thesis
A Lucky share above about Rs35bn: 64% of FY26 operating cash flow, about one year of free cash flow, 45% of standalone net cash. Also a price above 8.7 times profit (earnings yield below the 11.5% policy rate), whatever the size. The card's trigger of Rs50bn is 91% of FY26 operating cash flow and is too loose; I recommend Rs35bn. Below Rs20bn (36% of OCF) it is noise.
