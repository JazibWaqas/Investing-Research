Written 18 Oct 2026 from the repo's data. Catalogue type 1; extends `knowledge/situations/02`. (K) = public on the date given; (H) = hindsight.

**Definitions.** Low = KSE-100 close 27,228.80 on 25 Mar 2020, 37.0% under the 43,218.67 close of 13 Jan. KSE-100 and KMI All Share are the repo's total-return files. Liquid = 60-session median daily traded value above Rs5m, over every name with prices (88 on the low day; the daily table alone has 71). What mattered. Four things were public before the low and none marked it. The 11 Mar auction had already priced a cut. The 17 Mar statement (K) called the volatility "externally driven" and put SBP reserves at $12.76bn, with net buffers "more than sufficient to cope with any portfolio outflows". The current account was narrowing and oil had halved. Against that, reserves fell $2.1bn from 6 Mar to 3 Apr and USD/PKR rose 5% in the three sessions after 24 Mar, so on the day the external account did not yet look safe.

## 5. What a reader applying the reflex would have done

The reflex (HOW_TO_THINK section 12) runs five checks. At +1 week (1 Apr, K) the regime was a shock with a backstop (IMF review agreed, 225bp of cuts, CPI falling, reserves $11.2bn on 27 Mar, bill 30bp over policy), type 1, no liquid name at a 252-high, the index 8.4% off the low and under its 50-day. The rule joins on the second signal, so it waits; that signal closed on 3 Apr. At +4 weeks (22 Apr, K): bill 100bp under policy, six names at 252-highs, three in KMI (KOHC, CHCC, FEROZ), cement the sector tide. At +8 weeks (20 May, K): ten names, six in KMI; breadth 85% and 57%.

Reading it. Waiting eight weeks cost 16 points of index return (50.6 against 34.3), or 6 against the knowable 3 Apr entry. The 252-high filter added nothing at +8 weeks (median +35% on ten names against +34% for the index). The money was in sector and name: from 20 May the tech basket made +293% (TRG +577%, SYS +216%, NETSOL +232%, AVN +159%), refiners +156%, steel +88%, cement +59%, banks +22%. Late highs in small names (SPEL, MACFL, AGP, HUMNL) returned -34% to -2%.

## 6. If-then rules this turn supports

Six lows are compared: 16 Aug 2019, 25 Mar 2020, 17 Jan 2023, 23 Jun 2023, 8 May 2025, 9 Mar 2026. The two 2023 lows are one regime, so there are five independent turns. 2026 has no full 12 months. Tags follow the catalogue.

| If | Then | Shared by | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| The index is 12% or more under its 252-high and under 7% of liquid names close above their 50-session average | A low is at hand. It does not name the day (2020: the under-7% zone lasted 11 sessions, 18 Mar to 1 Apr, with the low in the middle) | The low day itself met this in 5 of 6 lows (not 23 Jun 2023: 18%, and only 8% off its high) | few |
| Two sessions after such a washout, fewer than 10% of liquid names close at a fresh 20-session low | The low is in. If most still are, it is not (19 Mar: 97%) | 5 of 6 lows (not 17 Jan 2023, 19%); in 2020 it separated the real low from the one false low that had a washout | few |
| The first rate cut or IMF agreement or approval has just been announced | Do not treat it as the entry: the buyer at that headline was then 9.3% to 17.6% lower at the low | 4 of 6 lows (2019 Board 3 Jul, 2020 cut 17 Mar, Jan 2023 agreement 14 Jul 2022 as dated in situation 03, 2025 cut 5 May); the headline came after the low in Jun 2023 and Mar 2026 | few |
| A calendar week makes a higher low and a higher close than the low week | Buy at its close, hold under the regime-reversal exit | 5 of 5 completed lows ended 12 months positive, +38% to +89%, worst close 0% to -8.2%; 2026 is -0.5% so far | few |
| You are waiting for the index's first close above its 50-session average | It costs return: 26 sessions after the low in 2020, 10 points less than the first-week entry | Worse in 4 of 5 completed lows (2025 the exception, by 5.6 points) | few |
| You are waiting for foreign buying | Do not: foreigners sold for 20 sessions after the low (2020 -$74m, 2025 -$33m, 2026 -$33m) | 3 of 6 lows, all three shocks; mutual funds sold into the low in 6 of 6; banks and DFIs bought at the three shock lows | few |
| USD/PKR jumps 5% in three sessions inside a shock | USD earners lead: tech +87% in 8 weeks against conventional banks +7% | 2020 only | one |
| A sector's input cost drops by a quarter or more | Its leader's 252-high comes first: cement was 4 of the first 10, coal -31% | 2020 only | one |
| You buy a 252-high in the first eight weeks | Expect the index, not more; pick by sector and by KMI-eligible leader | 2020 only; 33% of 94 first highs gained another 50% in 126 sessions against 25.6% over all years | one |

## 7. Caveats

- Universe. The daily table omits conventional banks, TRG and other names without a `prices_raw` file; I added them from `data/panel_close*.parquet` (seed prices) with the same Rs5m test. ENGRO, FFBL, PIAA and PSMC have no panel price and use the daily table's return chain. 786 is excluded as a stale quote.
- Inflation. `cpi.csv` for Nov 2019 to May 2020 runs 0.3 to 1.1 points above the figures the SBP statements quote (13.68, 13.74, 14.89, 12.94, 8.57 against 12.7, 12.6, 14.6, 12.4, 8.2); the table uses the SBP figures. March and April exist only in the seed series (11.38, 9.09).
- Dates. `imf_events.csv` has no 2020 rows; the second-review agreement comes from the 17 Mar statement. The April IMF loan and items marked "recalled" come from memory or `events_shocks.csv` status R. Publication times of auctions and flows were not checked. Intraday columns are used as given (the close sat inside the day's range on every 2020 row).
- Samples. 15 leaders, five independent turns, one incomplete; every 12-month figure is conditional on a rising market. Numbers were computed in a session scratchpad, not saved as a script.
