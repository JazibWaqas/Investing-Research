SGPL has become Crestwell Healthcare and is no longer power. For a KMI-only investor the tradeable sector is effectively HUBC and KEL; the rest are ineligible or too illiquid.

## 1. How this sector makes money

1. **PPA terms.** Under take-or-pay a plant earns its capacity payment whether or not it runs. Islamabad terminated five PPAs in Oct 2024 (Hub plant, Lalpir, Atlas, Saba, Rousch) and moved many others to take-and-pay, where only dispatched units earn the return. Terms decide whether revenue exists at all.
2. **Circular debt cash flow.** Profit is booked against CPPA-G receivables, so dividends depend on collection. The levers are the stock against the IMF ceiling, the Rs1.225tn bank facility (repaid through a Rs3.23/kWh surcharge) and late-payment-interest waivers (Rs387bn waived).
3. **Dispatch and fuel.** On take-and-pay plants, profit is utilisation times margin. Merit order runs hydel, coal, nuclear, wind, then gas, RLNG and furnace oil. Qatar's force majeure and the Hormuz closure cut RLNG output sharply from March 2026: RLNG plants such as NPL lose, imported-coal plants such as CPHGC and Narowal gain.
4. **Dividends from associates and diversification.** HUBC earns mostly through CPHGC, Thar Energy, ThalNova, Prime E&P and BYD. NPL and NCPL earn through NexGen Auto (Jaecoo). Holding-company dividend flow matters more than plant revenue.
5. **Regulation and rates.** NEPRA determinations drive KE (multi-year tariff, subsidy, write-offs). Interest rates drive finance cost and the P/E the market pays. In this repo's 2017 to 2026 test, power led in hike regimes and lagged after first cuts.
