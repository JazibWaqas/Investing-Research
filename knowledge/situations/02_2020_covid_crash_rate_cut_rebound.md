# Situation 02: March 2020 crash and the rate-cut rebound

Written 2026-10-18. Type: shock and rebound with a policy response. Sources: `data/macro/` (kse100, policy_rate_decisions, usdpkr, cpi, tbill_auctions), `data/panel_close_adj_pricereturn.parquet` (51 liquid survivors), `tests/rate_cycle_episodes.md`, `knowledge/sectors/tech.md`. Items marked "asserted" come from search snippets (Dawn, Business Recorder, ProPakistani, ARY) or general knowledge and were not opened in full.

## Dates and regime

- Policy rate 13.25% since July 2019. CPI 14.9% in Jan 2020, 12.9% in Feb (observed). 3-month T-bill 13.5% on 13 Jan. USD/PKR 154.8.
- IMF: the 2019 programme was running. KSE at 43,219 on 13 Jan 2020, the high of the post-2019 recovery (case 01).
- Pakistan first COVID cases: late February 2020 (asserted, general knowledge).

## What was knowable, and when

1. 21 Feb to 16 Mar: the global sell-off. KSE fell from 40,249 to 33,685 (-16%; observed).
2. 17 Mar: a scheduled MPC cut of 75bp to 12.5%, the first cut in four years (Dawn, asserted). KSE closed 32,617 and kept falling.
3. 24 Mar: an emergency MPC cut of 150bp to 11.0% (observed; ARY and ProPakistani asserted). KSE closed 28,565.
4. 25 Mar: KSE 27,229, the low, -37.0% from 13 Jan.
5. 16 Apr: a further 200bp to 9.0% (observed; Dawn calls it an emergent cut). By 25 Jun: 7.0%. Cumulative 625bp in 100 days while CPI fell from 11.4% (Mar) to 8.6% (May).

The cuts were announced before the low. The first link (rates) was strong and large, but the index did not turn on the first two.

## What the market did

| Date | KSE | vs low |
|---|---|---|
| 17 Mar close | 32,617 | +19.8% |
| 24 Mar close | 28,565 | +4.9% |
| 25 Mar close | 27,229 | low |
| 17 Apr close | 32,832 | +20.6% |
| 17 Dec 2020 | 43,767 | +60.7% |
| 14 Jun 2021 | 48,726 | +78.9% (high) |

From 25 Mar to 17 Dec the median of 51 liquid survivors rose 104%. Leaders: NETSOL +527%, TRG +480%, GHNI +428%, AVN +420%, NRL +360%, PIOC +288% (survivors only). In the first seven weeks (to 15 May) the leaders were PIOC +159%, CHCC +89%, AVN +87%, TRG +85%; median +36%.

## The right move

The cut was the signal, not the entry. A buyer at the 17 Mar close (32,617, after the first cut) was 12.4% down at 24 Mar and would be out on a 10% stop at 28,565. A buyer after the 16 Apr cut (31,329) held until a 10% trailing stop on 11 Oct 2021 (43,829): +39.9%. A buyer at the low (unknowable) got +61% on the same exit. A staged approach (a third on 17 Mar, a third on 24 Mar, a third after a close above the 17 Mar level) averages roughly 31,000 and ends near +40% (arithmetic from the table, approximate). The stop is a placeholder, not a tested rule.

Sector choice mattered more than timing: the PKR moved 154.8 to 166.8 (observed), the 3-month bill fell from 13.5% to 7.1%, and USD earners in tech (NETSOL, TRG, AVN) rose 4 to 6x. `knowledge/sectors/tech.md` documents NETSOL +1,051% to 3 Mar 2021 on a raw basis.

## The trap

Buying the first cut as the low. Two further traps: the policy response was in the first days of volatility, so the rebound began on a day with no news; and after +60% the investor who had sat out chased in June 2021 near the high (48,726), which later cost a 10% drawdown.

## Situation type and lesson

Shock with a policy backstop. Pattern: the central bank cuts, the index keeps falling for one to two weeks, then rises 20% in three weeks. Rule drawn: size the first entry for the chance it is the wrong week (a third), add on confirmation, and pick where the policy transmits (USD earners, cyclicals) rather than the index. Compare case 06 (May 2025), where the rebound arrived as a weekend gap that could not be bought.

## Questions that would have caught it

- Q1 Regime: is the shock external (virus) or domestic (balance of payments)? In 2020 the external accounts were improving: the rupee was stable and the IMF programme was in place.
- Q2 Type and base rate: policy backstop after a shock. n = 1 here; the other shock cases are 06 and the March 2026 war.
- Q3 Actors: who gains from a rate collapse? Leveraged cyclicals and USD earners.
- Q5 Exit and loss: how much can be lost if the first buy is the wrong week? A third of the intended size.
