# Situation 04: the June 2023 IMF standby and the 2023 to 2024 rally

Written 2026-10-18. Type: regime bottom with a confirmed trigger. Sources: `data/macro/`, `data/panel_close_adj_pricereturn.parquet` (40 liquid survivors), `tests/rate_cycle_episodes.md`, `knowledge/lessons.md` items 2, 9, 13. News: IMF press release 23/261 (12 Jul 2023), Friday Times and Al Jazeera 12 Jul 2023, ProPakistani 10 May 2023 (asserted from search snippets, not opened in full).

## Dates and regime

- Policy rate 21% (4 Apr), 22% (26 Jun 2023, the cycle peak). 3-month T-bill 22.0% (observed).
- CPI 37.97% in May 2023, the peak; 29.4% in June (observed).
- USD/PKR 286.5 on 23 Jun, after the late-January float moved it from 228 to 268.
- KSE 40,065 on 23 Jun 2023 (the low). Reserves were near default levels, and Bloomberg said the arrest of Imran Khan on 9 May moved Pakistan closer to default (asserted).

## What was knowable, and when

1. 26 Jun: the SBP hiked 100bp to 22% and KSE rose 3.4% to 41,437, with the IMF-aligned FY24 budget just passed (budget date asserted).
2. 29 Jun (some sources say 30 Jun): staff-level agreement on a nine-month, $3bn Stand-By Arrangement (asserted). Trading resumed on 3 Jul with KSE at 43,899, +5.9% on 27 Jun.
3. 12 Jul: the IMF Board approved $3bn (SDR 2,250m), immediate disbursement $1.2bn (IMF, asserted). KSE 45,515; USD/PKR 277.6.
4. By Nov: CPI had fallen to 29.2%, and the SBP held at 22% from Jun 2023 until it cut on 10 Jun 2024.

## The right move

The entry that existed was 3 Jul (43,899) or the Board date, 12 Jul (45,515). With a 10% trailing stop on closes, a 12 Jul entry exits on 26 Dec at 59,171 (+30.0%) and a 29 Jun entry exits there at +34.8%. Holding to 16 Dec 2024 was +155% from 12 Jul 2023. The stop is a placeholder, not a tested rule. The cost of waiting for the Board rather than buying the SLA window was 3.7% (45,515 against 43,899); the cost of waiting until the low was obvious was larger, since 31 Jul was already 19.9% above it.

Sector choice: the first six months were led by refiners and E&Ps, plus the speculative AIRLINK. With the bill at 22%, equities needed 11% over the half-year to break even against cash, and the median liquid stock beat that by 40 points.

## The trap

Two. First, waiting for rate cuts: the SBP did not cut until 10 Jun 2024, and the index had gained 83% by then (40,065 to 73,253). A rule that required cuts first missed the largest leg. Second, valuation: PSX traded at 3 to 4x earnings (lesson 2), which was cheap against the crisis multiple but not a constant. The right comparison was relative P/E and the direction of the regime.

## Situation type and lesson

IMF trigger at the end of a hike cycle. What marked it: CPI past its peak (May), the policy rate at its peak with a hold coming, a budget the IMF accepted, a staff-level agreement, and a dated Board meeting. Four public facts inside 17 days. Contrast case 01, where the index fell for six weeks after approval; here the low came before the SLA. Rule drawn: when the Board date is public and the budget and rupee are aligned, enter in stages and do not wait for a cut.

Limits: the Board date was not the low in any of the three IMF cases (2019: the low came six weeks later; 2022: five months later; 2023: eleven days earlier). n = 3 is too small to quote odds, but it argues against waiting for the Board.

## Questions that would have caught it

- Q1 Regime: is CPI past its peak? Is the policy rate at its peak? Is the budget IMF-compatible? Has the rupee stopped falling?
- Q2 Situation type: IMF Board pending with a dated meeting. Base rate: n = 3 across cases 01, 03 and 04.
- Q4 Price confirmation: a 6% gain within a week of the SLA, from an 18-month base around 40,000, is confirmation rather than extension.
- Q6 Traps: stale absolute P/E, and "wait for the cut".
