# Situation 06: the May 2025 India conflict, crash and rebound

Written 2026-10-18. Type: geopolitical shock and rebound. Sources: `data/macro/kse100.csv` (open, high, low, close), `data/panel_close_adj_pricereturn.parquet` (108 liquid survivors), `data/macro/policy_rate_decisions.csv`. News: IMF press release 25/137 (9 May 2025); Business Recorder, Tribune, Arab News, Daily Pakistan, Business Standard on 12 May 2025 (asserted from search snippets, not opened in full). The Pahalgam attack date (22 Apr) and the 7 May strikes are general knowledge, not re-fetched.

## Dates and regime

- Policy rate 12.0% until 5 May, then cut 100bp to 11.0% on 5 May (observed). CPI 0.7% in March and 0.3% in April. USD/PKR 281. 3-month bill 12.0%.
- IMF: a 37-month EFF (approved 25 Sep 2024). The first review and a $1.4bn climate facility were due at the Board on 9 May.
- KSE 118,430 on 22 Apr, 4.3x the 2020 low and +196% from the 2023 low: a stretched index into a shock.

## What was knowable, and when

1. 22 Apr: the attack in Kashmir. KSE rose 0.04% that day, then fell on four of the next seven sessions (-1.0%, -1.9%, -1.2%, -3.1% on 30 Apr) and stood 3.6% lower on 2 May (observed).
2. 5 May: a 100bp rate cut; KSE flat.
3. 7 May: strikes began (general knowledge); KSE -3.1%, closing 110,009 after an intraday low of 107,008.
4. 8 May: KSE -5.9% to 103,527 (intraday low 101,599), a lower circuit (Business Standard, asserted). 12.6% below 22 Apr.
5. 9 May (Friday): the IMF Board approved about $1bn and the $1.4bn RSF (IMF PR 25/137). KSE +3.5% to 107,175, still before any ceasefire.
6. 10 May (Saturday): the ceasefire, announced by President Trump (asserted).
7. 12 May: KSE opened at 117,104 (+9.3% on the Friday close) and closed at 117,298, +9.45%, the largest one-day gain on record (Tribune, asserted), with a trading halt.

Breadth on the rebound (8 May to 12 May, 108 liquid names): median +13.7%, 25th to 75th percentile +11.3% to +16.6%, worst -7.0%, best PACE +34.8%, then CNERGY +29.7%, BML +26.9%. Banks and high-beta small caps led. No liquid name rebounded 50% in the first two sessions. The 8 May to 1 Jul window is another matter: median +27.1%, BNL +275%, BIPL +107%, FLYNG +81%, FABL +80%.

## The right move

There was no clean entry. The rebound was a weekend gap: the Monday open (117,104) was already 9.3% above the Friday close, so a Monday buyer got +0.2% to the close. The reachable entries were (a) the 8 May close, 103,527, or (b) the 9 May close, 107,175, after the IMF approval and before the ceasefire. Entry (b) returned +9.4% in one session and +19.6% to 1 Jul 2025. The best prepared action was not trading on the way down: sell nothing at -12% in a market that had a policy backstop (IMF and rate cuts) and a long gap-risk history; keep a cash balance able to buy on 9 May. A trailing stop from case 05 would have sold at 103,527, the low.

This is not a repeatable timing edge. What was repeatable: hold the core through a shock when the external account, the IMF and the rate path are all intact, and keep cash for a Friday.

## The trap

Selling at the low, and waiting for the ceasefire to buy. The price move happened between a Friday close and a Monday open. The other trap: reading a 6% fall as the start of a repricing, when it was shock pricing on an index that had risen about 196% in two years.

## Situation type and lesson

Geopolitical shock with intact fundamentals. The tell was what the market was reacting to: a fall of 12.6% in 11 sessions, with no change in rates, reserves, or the IMF programme (the IMF approved on the Friday). Contrast the March 2026 Hormuz shock, where oil moved 70% and earnings changed (case 07). The question for any shock is whether it changes cash flows or only sentiment.
