## Verdict

Buy the core on Monday. But the book as drawn claims more than the evidence gives it in two places, the TIGHT-regime sector list and the 40% satellite. Fix both by labelling and sizing, not by waiting.

## 1. Is this the right use of his risk appetite?

**The core rests on the weakest leg of S3.** Observed: splitting S3's 124 trades by sector list, the 16 trades in TIGHT-list names (E&P, Islamic banks, fertilizer) made Rs1.98m on Rs54.7m of cost, median trade +0.3%, hit rate 50%. The other 108 trades (EASING and NEUTRAL lists, SAZEW and LUCK among them) made Rs10.3m, median +5.9%, 57% hits. Observed also: the sector_momentum_regime test's regime-tilt rule has "no edge" (placebo p 0.255), and the three hike-window relatives in sector_map are +4/+9/+1 (E&P), +12/+21/−2 (fertilizer), +22/+4/+1 (Islamic banks). Reasoned: the "regime-favoured" label on the core does no work the evidence supports. The core is three large, liquid, cheap, dividend-paying index names, and PICKS.md's own expectation (index-like, 11 to 14%) is the honest one. That is fine; say so, and stop describing the regime as the reason to own them.

**Concentration.** S3 held 3 names, and that is where both the SAZEW gain and the Rs5.15m April 2026 loss came from (observed). Reasoned: for this investor concentration is cheap now. Deposits dominate the first five years (strategy.md), so a 50% drawdown on a Rs300k book is six weeks of deposits. Three names is right; five would hedge against nothing the evidence shows. But concentration pays only if the picks carry a stock-level edge, and none is measured (S3 value pick +5.5 pt on n 28, contradicted by S4 at +0.1; observed). So: three names, equal weight, no trimming before 35%, and accept that the result is mostly the sector.

**The overrides.** MEBL over FABL cites lesson 6, but the mechanical S3 bank rule already is lesson 6: it ranks banks on ROE/PB (observed, method section), and on that rule FABL (17/1.2 = 14) beats MEBL (34/3.3 = 10). The override contradicts the lesson it cites. It is a franchise judgement, and the quality-within-sector test is "weak" (p 0.054, three years). MEBL's red-team expectation is +3%; FABL's sector note says "mid-teens maybe," at P/E 6.4 and a higher yield. FFC over ENGROH is different: ENGROH's 40% ROE is flagged as a probable standalone/consolidated mix (lesson 1) and its holdco discount is 9%, so there is a data-quality reason. Recommendation: FABL as the bank, FFC, OGDC. The larger point: overriding two of three mechanical picks on day one means the live record tests neither the rule nor the judgement. Whichever book is not bought must run as a frozen paper portfolio, scored monthly in rupees.

**60/40.** Observed: both earnings satellites fail their own-pool placebo; red-team expected returns on the cards are +2 to +10%; the deals edge is "thin"; the critic calls the split "a risk intent, not a quota." Reasoned: with no measured satellite edge, 40% is a quota. Its expected cost is four weeks of cash per deposit plus trading costs; its expected benefit is zero until the scorecard says otherwise. The appetite for "win big" is better served where the only big outcomes in the data came from: holding a concentrated core through a sector move without stops. Set the satellite at 25% (one Rs12.5k ticket a month), keep the four-week rule, raise it only when the frozen-prediction scorecard shows a hit rate above the pool's after 10 or more trades. At 40%, the second deposit's "hold Rs20k for triggers" becomes a standing cash reserve, which is what lesson 13 warns against.

## 2. Buy Monday or wait?

Buy, for a reason the framing misses: the first Rs50k is about 1% of what he will deposit over eight years. The entry price of one deposit is nearly irrelevant; the habit is everything. The evidence on TIGHT regimes is uncomfortable and should be stated: KMI All Share TR returned −10.8% (2018), +6.6% (2019), +1.4% (2021), −9.3% (2022) in hike years when 6M bills paid 7 to 15% (observed, rebench year table), and after the two complete first hikes KSE100 was −5.6% at 120 days (observed). Bills beat the index in each of those years. The one TIGHT year that broke the pattern was 2023: policy rate 22%, index +53% (observed). With monthly deposits, missing that year was unrecoverable; waiting through 2022 saved about 9 points. The asymmetry decides it. He also will not hold cash and cannot buy bills in this mandate, so "wait" means idle brokerage cash, not 12%.

**Lesson 13.** Observed: its only citation is a ChatGPT comparison (Rs2.86m vs Rs4.26m) with no period, rules or file in the repo. As evidence it is worth little. But the conclusion stands on better ground than its citation: B0, the no-skill buy-and-hold, made 2.43x deposits, and the index IRR of 21.4% beat bills over the whole period, though only because of 2023 to 2024 (reasoned from the year table). Rewrite the lesson to cite the walk-forward, and add the caveat that in hike years the index has lost to bills.

## 3. The one piece of evidence

The TIGHT sector list. It picks all three core names. If it carries no edge, the core is three index-like names chosen by a story, which is survivable; if it carries a negative edge (fertilizer and E&P lagged the KMI by 3.5 to 4.4 points over six months, observed) the core is a mild underweight of what is working. The check takes an hour: from walkforward_trades.csv, the TIGHT-list trades' time-weighted return against KMI All Share TR over the same months, and an EW basket of the TIGHT sectors over every month the regime read TIGHT since 2018, against a placebo of three random sectors. I ran the first half: median +0.3%, 50% hit, n 16. If the full check shows nothing, keep the three names but drop the regime language, and let future deposits go to the cheapest of the twelve largest KMI names.

Second candidate: the +5.5 pt S3 value-pick figure (n 28), the only stock-level edge in the repo and the basis for the "61 to 66%" confidence language in PICKS.md. S4's identical rule gives +0.1. Treat it as zero until it is out of sample.

## 4. Three sentences for him

Put Rs30k into FABL (or MEBL if you prefer the franchise, knowing that is a taste call), OGDC and FFC on Monday and the same every month; these are big, cheap, dividend-paying names, and the plan is to hold them through a 40 to 50% fall without selling. Keep at most Rs12k a month for one dated bet with a written card and a price you will not chase, and if nothing qualifies in four weeks it goes into the core. Nothing we tested makes 30% a year; the one thing that reliably loses is stopping the deposits, so decide now that you will not.
