# Independent critic review: what the PSX program has not established

17 October 2026. Repository data through 16 October; linked public sources checked on 17 October. “Testable” means a retrospective test is possible, not that the data are complete.

## 1. Claims that do not follow from the evidence

**“Of every mechanical signal tested, none gives a retail investor a reliable long-only edge after costs”** (`DECISIONS.md:12`). The test suite does not support that universal negative. Its strongest system result, S3, ends at Rs17.63m against B0’s Rs12.42m and the KSE100’s Rs17.12m (`tests/system_walkforward.md`). S3 is in sample, concentrated in SAZEW and LUCK, and lost 24.6% in 2026, so it proves no deployable edge either. The correct statement is that the *specified and adequately measured* rules have not established a repeatable edge. Settle this with a genuinely frozen 2026 onward paper portfolio, or a historical holdout whose sector lists and security screens are chosen without using its returns, compared against an investable KMI benchmark after all costs.

**“Money is made by staying invested in a concentrated set of quality names plus dated stock-specific catalysts”** (`DECISIONS.md:12`). Quality is undefined in the system test, while the actual S3 core is a regime and value rule. The catalyst satellite adds no detectable value: S2’s median beats its random-name placebo by 0.42 percentage point, S4 trails by 0.20; the sponsor satellite is absent. The result does not validate discretionary catalyst selection. Number needed: incremental money-weighted and time-weighted return of a predeclared quality-plus-catalyst book against the same quality book without catalysts, net of fees, tax and cash drag, across independent catalyst episodes.

**“The refiners were flaggable ... as a trade from 8-9 Jul”** (`catalyst_trade.md:28`). That date follows a selected winner. The combined three-leg rule has never been applied to all eligible prior events, and none of the 2026 refiners qualifies for the separately tested H3 ignored-quarter definition (`tests/results_surprise_drift.md`). The stated “about 1 in 3” chance of a 50% gain (`catalyst_trade.md:66`) is judgment, not a base rate. Settle it with every historical leg-one candidate, timestamped leg-two and policy decisions, executable entry, 120-day gain frequency, median excess return and worst loss, clustered by policy episode.

**“The latest sensible entry” is the first 5x-volume day** (`catalyst_trade.md:38`). Signal 9’s 788 observations have a negative 60-day median of 3.4%, and observations are clustered in 219 names and common episodes. A better placebo alone does not identify a good purchase. Number needed: after-entry excess median and drawdown for the *combined* rule against matched names, sectors and dates, including the next executable ask price and failed fills.

## 2. Other ways money may be made, and concrete tests

**Quality at a reasonable price.** [Kashif et al. (2023)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10448074/) report abnormal PSX size and value returns. The repo tests earnings yield and ROE/PB, but not cash conversion, accruals or payout cover. EPS for 129 names permits a partial test; cash-flow statements must be parsed. Rank liquid KMI names within sector on lagged ROE and cash conversion, hold 12 months, and compare with simple value and KMI30, including removed names. NETSOL’s receivables show why (`NETSOL.md:28`).

**True size.** The [2023 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10448074/) claims small-firm premiums; `tests/size_liquidity.md` sorts on *traded value*, not capitalization. Partly testable after collecting historical shares: form KMI capitalization quintiles, constrain exits by participation rate, and compare annual total returns including delistings. The existing -0.45% monthly liquidity spread is not a size result.

**Dividend carry versus T-bills.** [JS Global (January 2026)](https://www.jsglobalonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/JS-Global-Pakistan-Equity-Outlook-2026.pdf) expects dividend growth to drive returns. [PSX](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/product-and-services/indices) ranks sustained payers in Dividend 20. The repo tested ex-day mechanics, not the carry portfolio. Testable after reconstructing pre-November 2024 dividends: rank KMI names on covered yield minus 6-month bill yield; report after-tax total and real returns, without double-counting ex-date adjustments.

**Screening-driven ownership changes.** [PSX’s 2022 guidelines](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/themes/psx/uploads/Guidelines_for_Shariah-Compliant_Investing_on_PSX.PDF) describe semiannual Shariah review; its [26 February 2026 notice](https://dps.psx.com.pk/download/attachment/271129-1.pdf) lowered the debt threshold from 37% to 33%. The recomposition study does not isolate threshold-bound firms. Testable with archived membership and statements: compare firms near the threshold with unaffected firms around announcement and effective dates. The rule change itself is one episode.

**Financing stress and forced selling.** [SECP’s daily market reporting](https://www.secp.gov.pk/document/daily-stock-market-report-dated-january-28-2026/?wpdmdl=63540) includes MTS activity, while [PSX’s market documents](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/product-and-services/indices) identify float and impact cost as central to tradability. Repo flows are by investor type and sector, so they cannot see stock-level leverage. New MTS open-interest and futures eligibility history are required. Test whether high MTS growth plus a market fall predicts next-week lower circuits and reversal in eligible KMI names, matched on size and sector; explicitly model inability to exit at a locked lower circuit.

**Information leakage, governance and the post-announcement gap.** [SECP’s 7 August 2025 complaint](https://www.secp.gov.pk/media-center/press-releases/secp-files-criminal-case-on-charges-of-insider-trading/) alleges pre-disclosure buying around a buyback and delisting; [its 6 March 2024 cases](https://www.secp.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Press-Release-March-6-SECP-files-criminal-cases-against-individuals-involved-in-stock-market-manipulation.pdf) describe cross-trades and high-priced orders. Allegations are not convictions and are not a licence to infer wrongdoing from volume. The repository’s daily prices, volume and announcement timestamps allow a test of abnormal turnover and pre-event return by filing type, against same-name quiet windows; order cancellations and account identity need unavailable order-level data. A separate legal, public-information rule could test buying only *after* a verified filing, with the actual opening gap and circuit constraints.

**IMF, politics and Ramadan.** The [IMF’s September 2024 approval](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2024/09/27/pr-24343-pakistan-imf-concludes-2024-aiv-consultation-pakistan-approves-37-mo-extended-arr) and [May 2025 review](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2025/05/09/pr-25137-pakistan-imf-completes-1st-rev-of-eff-arrang-and-approves-req-for-arrang-under-rsf) distinguish staff agreement from cash release. Test program-level staff, board, tranche and policy-condition events against sector returns; add a dated political-event ledger. The older [PIDE equity study](https://pide.org.pk/research/a-seasonality-in-the-pakistani-equity-market-the-ramadhan-effect/) found lower Ramadan volatility but no significant mean-return change; a [2023 study](https://jisrmsse.szabist.edu.pk/index.php/szabist/article/view/577) found no index return effect. Test Hijri Ramadan years on daily KMI returns, liquidity and spreads, rather than Gregorian months. Both need external event calendars.

## 3. Test-design errors to fix before drawing stronger conclusions

The most consequential error is the **benchmark label**. `strategy_candidates.md:27`, `system_walkforward.md` and `walkforward.py:563,680` call `data/macro/kse100.csv` “price only.” Its 16 October close is **166,419.72**, matching PSX’s KSE100, while PSX showed KSE100PR around **49,895**. [PSX’s June 2025 brochure](https://www.psx.com.pk/psx/themes/psx/uploads/KSE-100-and-KSE100-PR-Brochure-Jun-2025-Updated.pdf) explicitly defines KSE100 as total return and KSE100PR as price return. Thus the claim that missing stock dividends flatter strategies *against* this benchmark is backwards for earlier years. Even a corrected KSE100 remains non-investable under the user’s KMI restriction; report the official KMI All Share and KMI30 series, plus a replicable liquid-KMI portfolio, on matched cash-dividend and fee bases.

Survivorship is admitted but underweighted. `strategy_candidates.md:123` and `system_walkforward.md` use 456 names still listed on 16 October 2026; the FY ratio file covers 108 names picked on current liquidity. Rolling 2026 membership lists backward (`strategy_candidates.md:10,119`) does not restore bankrupt, merged or delisted losers. The universe, sector returns, placebos and quality inference all inherit this bias. Recover historical PSX listings and delisting proceeds before interpreting a 2018 to 2026 strategy contest.

The satellite placebo in `strategy_candidates.md:37` and `walkforward.py:609-621` draws random U(t) names on the signal’s entry and exit dates. It does not condition on EPS-data coverage, sector, liquidity, prior return, P/E or event eligibility; `walkforward.py:775` itself runs a separate EPS-name placebo. Same dates control market moves, but not selection. Match on observable pre-event features, preserve clustered events, and compare *portfolio paths*, including unused cash and replacement trades, not only median completed trades. Do not call a 0.42-point median difference a passed signal.

Episode counting remains inconsistent. The seed audit correctly reduces 13 cuts to three cycles (`seed_audit.md:19`) and 33 CPI months to ten runs (`:26`), but `catalyst_trade.md:59-66` treats 788 volume flags as though they convey 788 independent bets. Results H3 has 42 completed 120-session observations but only 12 seasons, and these overlap later earnings. Bootstrap by name, sector and calendar episode; show leave-one-crisis and leave-one-rerating results.

Formation and execution need an audit. `strategy_candidates.md:25` substitutes FYE+4 months for missing filings; 66 of 530 FY rows need this imputed date (`system_walkforward.md`). EPS consolidation is unverified. A one-month timing variant changes B1’s ratio from 1.056 to 1.461. `strategy_candidates.md:7` forms at month-end close and trades at next month’s first *close*, implicitly assuming a fill at a price known only afterward. Retest at next open. Costs are 0.25% per side (`:17`), with fractional shares and suspended names sellable. Model spreads, impact, tax, circuits and failed fills; Rs1.7m satellite tickets cannot be costed like Rs12k tickets.

## 4. The strategy frame: what I would actually do

For Rs50k now and monthly, **invest on schedule**. Put roughly Rs25k per deposit into liquid KMI names selected on profitability, cash conversion, balance-sheet strength and dividend cover, with sector limits. Reserve Rs25k for one or two upside bets only when a dated change has an estimated payoff, probability, failure price and deadline. Otherwise buy the core with it. Cap each name near 25% and count correlated oil/rates/circular-debt exposures together. The 50/50 split is a risk intent, not a quota forcing bad trades.

Do not buy NETSOL or PSO just because results approach: their cards estimate about +2% and +3.8% net, with larger cash-conversion and receivables uncertainties. EPCL’s tender spread faces pro-rata and failure risk; its terms remain unverified. FFC, MEBL and an E&P name are plausible core candidates (`sector_map.md:32`), not a validated portfolio. Check KMI eligibility before ordering. Waiting for perfect prices costs money (`lessons.md:17`); a calendar date alone is no buy signal.

My disagreement with the emerging conclusion is precise: the null results reject several **implementations**, not mechanical long-only investing as a class. Dividend carry versus bills, cash-flow quality, capitalization-weighted KMI exposure, Shariah threshold events and leverage unwinds were never cleanly tested. Equally, discretion around a well-told refiner story has less measured support than the authors imply. Until the accounting and benchmark errors are fixed, disciplined monthly investment is the default, and catalyst bets are experiments with frozen forecasts and small, explicit loss budgets.

## 5. Five highest-value next actions, ranked

1. **Rebuild the point-in-time total-return panel and benchmarks.** Add delisted names, complete dividends, filing dates and official KMI/KSE series; reconcile the 16 October KSE100 label. Expected payoff: removes the largest directional biases from every result and may reverse strategy rankings.
2. **Run a simple investable KMI contest.** Compare cap-weighted KMI, KMI30, monthly liquid equal-weight, quality plus yield, and scheduled contributions with identical fees and Shariah exits. Expected payoff: establishes the actual opportunity cost for a Rs50k/month investor before adding complex signals.
3. **Test quality, dividend carry and true size jointly.** Parse cash flows and historical shares; freeze screens and compare 2018-21 with 2022-26 by independent year. Expected payoff: locates a durable core engine, if one exists, rather than assuming “quality” works.
4. **Audit catalyst cases as a complete opportunity set.** Collect all announced deals including failures, all ignored quarters and all sponsor filings; predefine executable entries and event clusters. Expected payoff: turns seductive winners into measured probabilities and prevents another 2026-refiner hindsight story.
5. **Start a prospective, rupee-denominated scorecard now.** Freeze each core and satellite decision, benchmark alternative, expected value, fill, spread, tax and breaker before the next deposit. Expected payoff: an honest out-of-sample record and a fast way to stop paying for false edges.

## Strongest objections in 200 words

The research has done falsification, but its synthesis outruns its evidence. It says mechanical long-only edges have failed while its best mechanical portfolio, S3, beats the buy-market baseline by 42%. That portfolio is not ready to trade: sector rules were selected from the same history, two stocks generated most profit, and 2026 has been brutal. Those defects require an out-of-sample test, not a pivot to untested discretionary catalysts. The catalyst satellite adds no convincing value, sponsor buying was not implemented, and the 2026 refiner rule was defined after seeing the winner. Its claimed one-in-three chance of a 50% gain is unmeasured. “Quality” has no operational definition in the backtest. The program calls its KSE100 input a price-only index even though the observed level and PSX methodology identify it as total return. That reverses its stated dividend-comparison bias. Survivor prices, incomplete dividends, imputed filing dates and frictionless execution infect the results. For this investor, monthly deployment into a Shariah-compliant core is defensible. A concentrated upside sleeve is defensible only as small, priced experiments. First build the investable KMI benchmark and measure quality, dividend carry, deal failures and catalyst episodes. The strongest conclusion is uncertainty, not the discovery of a veteran’s formula.
