# Peer review — 18 October 2026

Evidence: only `knowledge/HOW_TO_THINK.md`, `tests/replay_pilot_postmortem.md`, and a skim of `tests/FACTS.md`, dated 18 October 2026. No new measurements.

**(a) History can test components of judgment; this replay cannot certify investment skill.**

“Every replay measures memory” overstates the finding. Memory, reasoning and instructions can operate together; contamination prevents identifying their contributions. Fixing the brief is useful engineering, but rerunning known outcomes remains development.

- **Adversarial masking:** replace names, dates, units and distinctive prose while preserving economic relationships. Have an evaluator attempt identification; compare decisions across equivalent disguises. Cost: substantial construction and auditing; masking removes institutional context. This tests identity dependence and presentation robustness. Failed recognition cannot exclude implicit memory, so returns remain contaminated evidence.
- **Synthetic paired situations:** change one decisive fact: debt maturity, dilution, earnings quality, deal probability, or entry price. Predefine the expected direction of exposure change. Cost: expert construction and realistic accounting; generators can encode their designers' prejudices. These test causal sensitivity, consistency and sizing discipline. Simulated profits prove only performance inside the assumed world.
- **Frozen older models:** use an immutable model artifact released before events, without retrieval. This is stronger than a declared cutoff or failed recall probe. Cost: availability, capability differences, and rebuilding historical packs and base rates. It tests that older model on subsequent history. A method rewritten after seeing outcomes still contaminates the experiment; cross-model differences do not isolate judgment.
- **Sequential disclosure and ablation:** reveal historical evidence in stages; freeze probabilities and allocations after each. Compare the same model with a minimal brief; reorder equivalent evidence or remove decisive items. Cost: modest, plus repeated runs. This tests sensible updating and whether the method changes behaviour. Neither disclosure order nor ablation removes remembered outcomes or validates forecasting skill.

Prioritise paired cases and disclosure tests as debugging tools. Keep prospective investment performance as the exam. Months of overlapping weekly outcomes in one regime will still not establish transfer across regimes.

**(b) HOW_TO_THINK confuses preferences, hypotheses and established facts.**

Youth and future deposits increase loss-bearing capacity; they do not make bad bets cheap. Stops cannot guarantee maximum losses: gaps, locks and liquidity can defeat them. “All-in within the stop budget” can contradict the budget. Shared sector and macro exposures matter without position-count limits.

Growth is not an investment return without entry valuation, dilution and cash reaching shareholders. Rejecting yield because the rupee payment is small confuses income preference with total return. The stated long-run business appreciation also falls well short of the desired annual compounding. Ambition must not become an assumed opportunity set.

“A surprise that cannot be diagnosed is rare” invites hindsight storytelling. Price reaction aggregates positioning, constraints and noise as well as information. Record rival explanations and discriminating observations; permit “unknown.” A historical winner is not automatically evidence that buying it was justified.

The ban on arbitrary numbers conflicts with the earnings-growth threshold and one-third profit-taking rule. Risk limits are policy choices, not empirical discoveries: label and freeze them. Different situations need different exits. Rejecting mechanical strategies on principle also prejudges the comparison; S3 deserves to remain a serious challenger.

Finally, the postmortem credits T-bill income despite a stocks-only, religiously constrained mandate. An unavailable return can be an external comparator, but cannot be booked as executable portfolio income. The growth core itself remains unvalidated in FACTS.

**(c) Make the agent accountable for allocating capital.**

Separate “not tested,” “imprecisely estimated,” and “evidence against.” None means automatic cash; none licenses an invented edge. Multiple weak signals sharing one cause are not independent confirmations.

Specify an investible default portfolio, including permissible cash, with uncertainty disclosed. Require deviations to beat the next-best eligible use of capital on expected payoff, horizon and stressed loss. Cash faces that comparison too. Do not force trades or fabricate precision to satisfy the posture.

Each situation card should specify the causal mechanism, what price already implies, relevant base-rate limitations, confirming evidence, falsifier, entry valuation, execution constraints and exit. Require a decision—buy, add, hold, reduce, exit or pass—with an amount and a reason. A watch item must name the observation that would change the decision.

Use different underwriting and exit logic for compounders, shocks, deals and momentum. Size against stressed portfolio losses and correlated exposures, not stop distance alone. Freeze preferences, method version and benchmark comparisons before observing outcomes. Score omissions alongside commissions, using a prospectively defined candidate set; otherwise every subsequent winner becomes an imaginary missed trade.

**(d) The trustworthy answer to the investor:**

We have not demonstrated that this AI judgment method adds value; the replay is invalid as a skill test and reveals excessive caution, while the simpler breakout comparator remains competitive. We can offer an auditable allocation process with explicit experimental risk limits, but neither your return target nor transfer across regimes is established. Future frozen decisions must earn those claims.
