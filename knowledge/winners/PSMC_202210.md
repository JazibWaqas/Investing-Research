# Winner: PSMC, 2022 to 2024

Written 2026-10-18. Sources: `data/prices_delisted/PSMC.csv` (raw, no corporate action), `data/announcements.parquet`, PSX document 218197 (19 Oct 2023, read as an image), Profit (17 Jan 2024, opened) and ProPakistani (12 Feb 2024, opened), Carspirit and ProPakistani on the Rs609 price (via search). Not a KMI name, so not buyable under the mandate; the shape is what matters.

## Dates and regime

Policy rate 22% from Jun 2023 to 29 Apr 2024. Pakistani car makers were shut for long stretches by import limits: PSMC filed 29 plant-shutdown notices between Aug 2022 and Dec 2023 and a material-information filing on 20 Mar 2023 about unpaid foreign-currency liabilities. The price reached Rs95.85 on 1 Jun 2023, 43% below the start. KMI All Share was +9.4% from 12 Oct to 3 Nov 2023.

## What was knowable and when

- Aug 2023: Chase Securities buy recommendation at about Rs100 (Profit, 17 Jan 2024).
- 11 and 12 Oct 2023: volume 0.7m and 1.4m shares against about 0.1m; price +2.6% and +5.5%. Nothing filed.
- 19 Oct 2023, board met at 10:00; filing at 13:47: the board resolved to delist under rule 5.14 and Suzuki Motor Corporation would be authorised to buy back minority shares at a price set by PSX or SECP. This was a material-information letter, but the data classes it "other" (title "Extract of resolution ... for delisting").
- 4 Dec 2023: formal application for voluntary delisting.
- 12 Jan 2024: a holder disclosed crossing 10%. 15 Jan: PSX fixed the minimum buyback price at Rs609, against about Rs406 proposed by the company (the Rs406 is inferred from "50% above", unverified). 18 Jan: Suzuki accepted Rs609 for at least 13.9m shares (62.84% of the 22.1m minority shares). 12 Feb: EOGM; holders of 15.39% voted against and threatened legal action.

## How the move unfolded

The first sign, 13 Oct, was a limit-up close (the limit was 7.5%) of Rs154.20 on 21,000 shares. Then 16 closes at the limit from 13 Oct to 3 Nov, Rs143.44 to Rs456.29, 3.2x, almost all on 10,000 to 55,000 shares a day; the exceptions were 19 Oct (675,000, the board day), 27 Oct (526,000) and 30 Oct (252,000). The board filing arrived on the fifth limit day, at Rs205.94. On 6 Nov the pattern broke: 4.3m shares, an intraday high of Rs490.51, a close of Rs438.26. The price then traded Rs405 to Rs585 until the end of December. From 4 Jan 2024 five more limit-ups lifted it from Rs625.91 to Rs898.57 on 11 Jan (the price of holders hoping for more than the floor). Then five closes down 7.1%, 7.2%, 7.5%, 7.5% and 7.5% to Rs609.00 on 19 Jan, and a flat line at Rs609 until the last trade on 25 Apr 2024.

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

A buyer on 19 Oct (the board-filing close, Rs205.94; bought next day at Rs221.39) selling at the Rs609 floor made 2.75x gross, or 3.2x with the 20% trailing stop that fired on 16 Jan at Rs716.51; about Rs222,000 net on Rs100,000, 452 shares. A buyer on 27 Oct (Rs341.67) made 2.1x. A buyer at the 6 Nov reversal (Rs438) made 1.6x. A buyer on 4 Jan (bought Rs672.85 next day) made 1.06x, and one on 10 Jan (Rs835.88) lost 14%. The January buyers were paying more than the highest price the sponsor was obliged to accept. The 12 days at the limit were hard to join at 21,000 shares a day, but 19 Oct had 675,000.

Exit was never a problem: 120 to 650 shares for Rs100,000, and the floor price had buyers.

## The trap

The spike to Rs898.57 after the price had already become a regulatory process. The library calls this an operator run because the delisting notice was not in the "material information" class; four limit closes (13 to 18 Oct) and a volume burst on 11 and 12 Oct preceded it, so the early lift looked like leakage (unverified, the pattern fits).

## The if-then rule

If a majority holder's board resolves to delist and buy out minorities, then the price converges on a regulator-set floor. Buy only before the floor is known if the price is under the sponsor's own offer; sell the position into the floor or on the first reversal day, and never pay above the last known offer.
