# Winner: Fauji Fertilizer, June 2023 to January 2026

Written 2026-10-18. Type: a fertilizer leader spared by the 2024 gas tariff change. Sources: `data/prices_raw/FFC.csv`, `data/winners_library.csv`, `data/announcements.parquet`, `data/fundamentals/quarterly_eps.csv`, `data/daily_table.parquet` (KMI flags), ProPakistani, Profit and Alfalah research via search (asserted). Multiples include dividends; FFC pays out heavily, which is why total return (9.48x) is far above price only (6.90x).

## Dates and regime

- 19 Jun 2023: policy rate 21% (22% a week later), CPI 29.4%, 3-month bill 22%, USD/PKR 287, KSE 40,621; the IMF Stand-By followed (case 04).
- 27 Jan 2026: rate 10.5%, CPI 5.8%, bill 9.9%, USD/PKR 280, KSE 188,203.

## What was knowable, and when

1. Quarterly EPS in the repo: 6.55 (Mar 2023), 9.48, 11.92, 8.67, 9.96 (Mar 2024), then 19.24 (Jun 2024, filed 30 Jul 2024 at 14:35), 17.72, 15.46, 12.23, 13.83, 17.24 (Sep 2025). Earnings roughly doubled and stayed there.
2. Feb 2024: the government raised the gas tariff for fertilizer plants on the SNGPL network, which spared Mari-network producers such as FFC; Engro raised urea by Rs882 a bag, FFC held Rs3,767 (ProPakistani 20 Feb, Profit 28 Feb 2024, asserted). 20 Mar 2024: an Alfalah note projected 2024 EPS up 55% to Rs36.1 (asserted). The FFC price was 1.2x to 1.4x of the start then.
3. Filings: 16 Oct 2024, acquisition of shares of Agritech; 9 Dec, a public offer for up to 151.05m Agritech shares; 4 Dec, the Lahore High Court judgment on the FFBL merger and 12 Dec the sanctioned scheme; 31 Dec, shares credited to FFBL holders (one FFC share for 4.29 FFBL; 150.87m new shares).
4. KMI: the derived lists show FFC out of the All Share index until the 2 Dec 2025 snapshot. Unverified, but it would have made FFC unavailable to this investor for 29 of the 31 months.

## How the move unfolded

Nothing for four months: 0.98x to 1.09x to October 2023. The first 252-high came on 11 Oct 2023 at 1.06x. Then a drift: 1.2x on 20 Feb 2024, 1.5x on 8 Apr, 2.0x on 2 Jul, 2.11x after the 30 Jul results. The pace then rose: 3.04x on 3 Oct (+8.2%), 4.07x on 21 Nov (+10%), 4.81x on 12 Dec, 5.25x on 16 Dec 2024, as the court approved the merger and the Agritech offer ran. Then six quiet months at 4.6x to 5.3x with a 15% dip to 8 May 2025.

From June: 5.3x, 6.2x in July, 6.8x in October, 8.3x in November on volume of Rs1.6bn a day, 8.3x in January. 9.17x on 23 Jan 2026, a high of 9.48x on 27 Jan, then two 10% down closes (29 Jan, 2 Mar) and a 29% fall to 6.75x on 9 Mar (the Hormuz closure). 8.03x on 16 Oct 2026. To the FFC peak, FATIMA made 6.6x, EFERT 3.3x and ENGROH 2.7x; EFERT, a KMI member, ran 3.0x from 24 Mar 2023 to 3 Jan 2025.

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

Ahead of each entry (log share): 1.06x on 11 Oct 2023, 97%; 1.25x on 4 Dec, 90%; 1.5x on 8 Apr 2024, 82%; 2x on 2 Jul, 69%; 3x on 3 Oct, 51%; 4x on 21 Nov, 38%; 5x on 16 Dec, 26%; 7x on 4 Nov 2025, 13%.

- First reasonable entry: 8 Apr 2024 at 1.51x, after the gas-tariff news and a first doubling of the quarterly EPS in sight; 2 Jul 2024 at 2.01x, with the Jun-quarter result at the end of the month, was still good.
- To stay in: the deepest dip before 2026 was 15%, so any trail of 25% or wider survived it. The 100-day-average rule left on 21 Apr 2025 (4.3x of an Oct 2023 entry) and missed a further doubling.
- Reasonable exit: a 25% trail (2 Mar 2026) or a 30% trail and later; or hold, since the earnings stayed doubled.
- Rs100,000 with dividends reinvested: from 11 Oct 2023, Rs662,000 (25% trail) and Rs758,000 held; from 8 Apr 2024 (1.51x), Rs465,000 and Rs533,000; from 2 Jul 2024 (2.01x), Rs348,000 and Rs398,000; from 3 Oct 2024 (3.04x), Rs230,000 and Rs264,000. The average-rule exit from 8 Apr 2024 gave Rs300,000. Exits chosen after the fact; n = 1; costs and dividend tax not deducted.
- Joining at about 7x (4 Nov 2025) paid 15% in eleven months.

## The trap

The four dead months and the 15% dip after 5x, where an average-based exit sold and the stock doubled again. The other trap is eligibility: the same chart in a name the investor could not hold. The KMI-eligible twins, EFERT and ENGROH, ran less (3.3x and 2.7x) and sat on the repriced gas network.

## Questions that would have caught it

- B2: the sector's real lever, here which gas network a plant sits on.
- B1, B11: five-year earnings power against the price; EPS doubled while the multiple stayed modest.
- B10: KMI status. For this investor the question came first.
- B6: payout and how the sponsor treats minorities in a merger (the FFBL swap ratio).

## The if-then rule

If a regulated lever splits a sector and one name's quarterly EPS doubles in its next filing, join on that result, hold with a 30 to 40% trail, and let earnings, not price, decide the exit; check KMI status first, and if the name is out, buy the nearest eligible peer.
