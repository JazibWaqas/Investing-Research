# Winner: CRTM, 2024 to 2026

Written 2026-10-18. Sources: price and volume panels, `data/announcements.parquet`, PSX documents 272716 (18 Mar 2026 reply) and 273040 (26 Mar 2026 disclosure), both read; `tests/winners_2026_postmortem.md`. No EPS rows for CRTM in `quarterly_eps.csv`, so earnings are not used. About 100m shares (10,988,709 shares were 10.99%), so the company was worth about Rs1.2bn at Rs12 and Rs8.8bn at the peak.

## Dates and regime

Policy rate 17.5% to 15% in the first months, 10.5% from 15 Dec 2025, hiked to 11.5% on 27 Apr 2026. The Gulf shock: KMI All Share fell from 72,510 (26 Jan 2026) to 59,625 (19 Mar), -18%, and was 65,474 on 16 Oct. Textile peers (NML, GATM, ILP, KTML, FML, DLL, AGTL) lost 6% to 25% from 26 Feb to 18 Mar; CRTM gained 239%.

## What was knowable and when

- 26 Jul 2024: PSX unusual-volume notice; 14 Mar to 16 Jun 2025: 16 insider-disclosure filings with direction not recorded in the data. No dividend, rights or results event in the window.
- 16 Mar 2026: PSX query. 18 Mar reply: "not aware of any undisclosed matter".
- 26 Mar, 14:37: Crescent Bahuman Limited (sponsors Nasir Shafi, Rizwan Shafi and family) had bought 9,200,000 voting shares on the open market, completed 19 Mar and settled 24 Mar; with Nasir and Rizwan Shafi their holding was 10.99%. The Section 110 filing came when the price was Rs49.67, 26% under the high.
- 18 May and 25 May 2026: director election and appointment of chairman and chief executive (titles only, content unverified).

## How the move unfolded

Sep 2024 to May 2025 was flat at Rs11.4 to Rs15.2 with brief lock days. The first sign was 16 Jun 2025, a new 252-day high (Rs17.58), then Rs23.13 on 23 Jun on 2.3m shares; PSX query 26 Jun. Seven months at Rs21.5 to Rs27.7, then -37% to Rs20.02 on 19 Feb 2026. From 27 Feb the price closed at the limit on 13 of 14 sessions to Rs67.53 on 18 Mar; 22m shares traded in 27 Feb to 19 Mar, so if the 9.2m bought all fell in that window the buyer was up to 42% of the volume (the filing says "over a period of time"). Four lower limits followed (19, 24, 25, 27 Mar) to Rs40.55, then a second leg without any filing: Rs41.29 on 31 Mar to Rs87.93 on 20 Apr (+113% in 14 sessions). After 21 Apr: -8.8%, a drift to Rs70 and, from June, almost no volume: median 2,588 shares a day from Aug to Oct, Rs58.20 on 16 Oct.

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

Entry on 3 Mar (Rs26.06 at the next close; the first limit close of the run was 27 Feb at Rs21.93) with a 20% stop exits 25 Mar at Rs49.23: 1.89x, about Rs88,000 net on Rs100,000 (3,837 shares). Entry on 5 Mar made 1.56x. After the disclosure, a signal on 26 Mar bought on 27 Mar at Rs44.70 exits 18 May at Rs70.10: 1.57x, +Rs56,000; 2 Apr made 1.34x; 9 Apr (Rs70.06) made nothing. The June 2025 entry made 1.19x only after 171 sessions and a 25% stop. The first two weeks were the last cheap fills; limit days can leave a buy order unfilled.

Exit risk grew after the peak: a Rs100,000 position (1,700 shares) was fine in May, but Rs1m would take weeks at 2,600 shares a day. A reader who held past May with no stop was in a name that had no buyers.

## The trap

Rising while the market and its sector fall looks like strength but was one buyer's order flow. The first leg ended when the buyer finished (19 Mar), four days before the filing that revealed it. Failure twins: CRTM's own June 2025 breakout (flat for seven months) and QUICE 2024.

## The if-then rule

If a thin KMI name locks up for several days while its sector and the index fall, assume a price-insensitive buyer: take at most a half position on day two or three with a 20% stop, and stop adding when a lower-limit close appears. When a sponsor-family filing names the buyer, the move can resume (here +113%), but treat it as a new, separate trade with its own stop and a position small enough to sell in one day of volume.
