# Winner: Faysal Bank (FABL), March 2023 to July 2026

Written 2026-10-18. Type: results jump that turned out to be a re-rating. Sources: `data/winners_library.csv`, `data/prices_raw/FABL.csv`, `data/panel_close_adj.parquet`, `data/announcements.parquet`, `data/fundamentals/quarterly_eps.csv` (not checked against filings), `data/macro/`, PSX documents 232436 and 251180 (scanned, read by eye), `data/index_members/`. Simulations: `tests/out/winners_results_jumps_sims.txt` (method in the ATRL case).

## Dates and regime

Library start 24 Mar 2023 (raw Rs21.57). Policy rate 20% (2 Mar), 21% (4 Apr), 22% (26 Jun), CPI 35.4%, USD/PKR 283, KMI All Share 19,050 (3.8x by July 2026; FABL 5.5x). Not a KMI All Share member in the derived snapshots until 10 Jul 2023, so a buyer under the investor's mandate could not have bought the March start.

## What was knowable, and when

1. 23 Feb 2023: year results, Q4 2022 EPS 2.38 (library: 1.41 for the comparable quarter, unverified). 28 Apr: Q1 EPS 2.18; the stock fell 6.0% that day on 5.5m shares.
2. Trailing EPS was Rs8.3 at Rs19.33 (adjusted), a P/E near 2.3, in a market on about 3.5x.
3. 3 Jul 2023: IMF session, lock-up +7.5%, but back to Rs21.41 the next day. 23 Nov and 6 Dec 2023: locks (7.2% and 7.1%); 5 Dec the first close above the prior 252-day high.
4. 7 Feb 2024, 12:26: Q4 2023 EPS 5.23 against 2.38, +120% (EPS file). The stock fell 2.9% that day (Rs32.14).
5. 30 May 2024: Ithmaar approved selling 75% of its 66.7% stake (about 50% of the bank) to GFH Financial Group (doc 232436). 31 May: Rs43.68 on 16.8m shares. 16 Apr 2025: talks terminated (doc 251180); the price did not move.

## How the move unfolded

Flat to up 20% until November 2023 (Rs21 to Rs28 raw), then the first 252-day high on 5 Dec 2023, 1.34x the low, with 83% of the log gain still to come. The EPS jump of 7 Feb 2024 was a non-event for the price (1.49x), the control-sale news added to it, and it peaked at Rs57.19 on 5 Jul 2024 (2.65x) with a policy cut cycle started on 10 Jun. It fell 22% to Rs44.50 on 3 Oct 2024, then climbed in steps: locks on 3 and 4 Jun 2025 (33m shares on 4 Jun) and 30 Jun to 1 Jul, Rs74 by 28 Aug 2025 and Rs104.05 on 6 Jan 2026. It fell 27% to Rs75.94 on 9 Mar 2026 (lower lock, the war shock) and made a last high of Rs101.56 on 3 Jul 2026; Rs91.49 on 16 Oct. EPS after Q4 2023 was 4.35, 4.58, 4.47, 2.33, 3.56, 3.30, 3.66, 4.55 and 3.51. Trailing EPS stayed near 13 to 16 while the price tripled, so trailing P/E went from about 2 to about 6.5 by January 2026. Still ahead: 4.1x at 5 Dec 2023, 3.7x at 7 Feb 2024, 2.7x at 31 May 2024, 1.5x in Aug 2025.

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

- First reasonable entry: the 5 Dec 2023 breakout, bought on 6 Dec near Rs28.7 raw. A 25% trail from highest close exited on 10 Mar 2026 at about Rs78.5 adjusted: 3.3x gross, plus 224% net. Rs50,000 became about Rs162,000; Rs200,000 about Rs648,000. A 30% trail was still holding on 16 Oct 2026 (plus 278%). A 20% trail left on 19 Sep 2024 at 1.6x and missed the second leg.
- Entering on the 7 Feb 2024 results instead: plus 201% with a 25% trail. The December entry never closed under cost; the February entry was 6% under at worst.
- Where joining late paid less: on 28 Aug 2025 (Rs67.7, 3.7x of the low), plus 15% with a 25% trail; on the 6 Jan 2026 high, minus 21% (stop) or minus 8% (30%), with a 25% drawdown in two months.
- What kept you in: 20% was too tight (this stock fell 22% and 27% inside a rising trend), earnings did not collapse, and the market re-rated banks as policy rates fell. The exit that mattered was a 25% fall from the highest close.

## The trap

The labelled "jump" did not repeat: Q4 2023 EPS was the high, and the next four quarters were lower. Buying it as earnings growth would have been right for the wrong reason, and a reader who exited when EPS fell (Q1 2024, filed 25 Apr 2024) would have left at about 1.4x the December entry. The takeover story (50% control sale) and its collapse did nothing to the price, so it was not a driver. The investor could not have bought the March 2023 start. Failure twins: banks or cyclicals with the same one-quarter spike at a high rate that did not re-rate: not tested here.

## Which questions would have caught it

A1 (policy rate and the first cut), B1 (one-off or trend: it was a peak quarter), B10 (KMI membership date), B11 (P/E near 2), C1 and C4 (breakout with locks and volume), C9 (a trail wide enough for 22% and 27% falls).

## The if-then rule

If a bank or exporter prints a strong quarter at a trailing P/E under about 3 after an IMF-type trigger, ignore the quarter and join on the first 252-day high that follows, with a 25 to 30% trail from the highest close, and do not sell on a fall in EPS alone. Untested outside that wave.
