# Winner: Mughal Iron and Steel, June 2019 to February 2022 (5.3x, a poor late payoff)

Written 2026-10-18. Sources: `data/winners_library.csv`, `data/prices_raw/MUGHAL.csv` (adjusted for the Nov 2021 bonus and a 16% rights issue; quoted prices ran Rs24.9 to about Rs110), `data/announcements.parquet`, `data/fundamentals/quarterly_eps.csv`, `data/macro/`. News (web search, snippets): Foundation Securities and Business Recorder notes on FY21 results; Profit on steel prices, 28 Nov 2021. Not a KMI name at the start; it is today.

## Dates and regime

Policy rate 12.25% at the start, 7.0% from Jun 2020, then back to 9.75% by the peak (hikes of 25bp, 150bp and 100bp on 20 Sep, 19 Nov and 14 Dec 2021). Rupee 163 to 174. KMI All Share +44% over the run. The library's five steel peers rose 71%.

## What was knowable, and when

- 24 Jun 2019: a "Clarification" filing, three days before the start. Quarterly progress reports on the use of an earlier rights issue run through 2019 to 2021 (a capex plan, not a result).
- Nov 2019 to Mar 2020: material information on 27 Mar and 11 May 2020 (bare titles).
- FY21 (June 2021): profit about Rs3.48bn, roughly 5x FY20, on copper-ingot sales and better rebar prices; analysts warned both were unlikely to last (Foundation Securities via search). Basic EPS: Sep 2021 5.03, Dec 2021 5.49, Mar 2022 2.50, so the quarter after the peak showed earnings halving.
- 19 Feb 2021: the board declared a 16% rights issue at Rs68 and an interim dividend; 12 Mar: the rights terms filed; 19 Mar: PSX "unusual movement" query; 4 Mar: a rated private sukuk. Price at the time: 4x the start.
- 1 Nov 2021: bonus shares credited. 17 Feb 2022: interim dividend.

## How the move unfolded

Choppy for two years. 1.5x in Nov 2019 (the first 252-day high came on 3 Jan 2020), 2x on 9 Mar 2020, a 47% fall in the Covid crash to 26 Mar and a recovery by 13 Jul. 3x on 31 Dec 2020, 4x on 22 Feb 2021 on turnover of Rs164m a day. Then eight months sideways with two 26% to 27% drops (Mar and Sep to Oct 2021), a 5x close on 2 Nov 2021 and a peak on 9 Feb 2022, eight weeks after the third hike of the cycle.

After the peak: 0.91x at 10 sessions, 0.75x at 20, 0.59x at 60, 0.47x at 250. 43 upper locks, the last in 2021.

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

Closing prices, 0.5% round trip, before tax.

- 3 Jan 2020 (first 252-day high, 1.85x): 3.0x ahead on paper, but -25% by the Covid exit with a 30% stop; with a 20% stop on 18 Mar, -15% (Rs100,000 left at about Rs85,000).
- 31 Dec 2020 (3x, Rs75.7): 1.79x still ahead. A 20% stop sold on 11 Mar 2021 at Rs93.8, +24%, about Rs123,000; a 30% stop sold on 28 Apr 2022 at +22%, about Rs121,000. The second 12 months of holding made nothing.
- 22 Feb 2021 (4x, after the rights news): no return left worth the risk.
- The trade here is that the first sensible entry lost, the second paid a quarter, and the rest of the hindsight multiple belonged to people who bought in 2019.

## The trap

A visible catalyst that was one-off and already fully reported: FY21 EPS at 5x, "unlikely to last". A rights issue declared while the price was 4x its start and 47% above the issue price. And a rate cycle turning: 275bp of hikes between Sep and Dec 2021, then the peak. Failure twins: the other steel names (the library's five-peer basket, up 71% on average) and, in 2021, the cement and steel names that bought inventory before prices rose.

## Questions that would have caught it

B1 (one-offs stripped: copper and inventory gains), B6 (how the sponsor treats minorities: rights at Rs68 into a Rs100 price, sukuk, bonus), A1 (the rate cycle turning), C10 (counting one episode as many: the steel boom was one story), C9 (the exit).

## The if-then rule this case suggests

If a sector name has already run 3x on a profit surge that analysts call temporary, and the company raises equity or the central bank starts a hiking cycle, take the +20% available and do not size it; a profit that is 5x its prior year without repeat is a sell signal here, not a chase.
