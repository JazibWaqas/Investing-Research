# Winner KEL, from June 2022: the takeover that was only a filing

Written 2026-10-18. Sources: `data/winners_library.csv`, price and volume panels (raw closes), `data/announcements.parquet` (titles; documents 189090 and 192791 not opened), `data/deals_history.csv`, `knowledge/situations/03_2021_22_hike_cycle_and_default_scare.md`, web searches on the Shanghai Electric withdrawals (Profit, Bloomberg Tax, ProPakistani; snippets only). Outcome window ends 30 Nov 2022 because the later KEL run (Dec 2022 onward, 3.7x) has a different cause.

## Dates and regime

Library start 3 Jun 2022 (Rs2.47), peak 17 Aug 2022 (Rs3.73, 1.51x, 48 sessions). Tier M (Rs14.7m a day), KMI member. Policy rate 12.25% in April, 13.75% in May, 15% on 7 Jul; rupee under pressure, default scare (situation 03). KMI All Share +7.7% from start to peak, so this was no wave. After the peak 27% was lost in 60 sessions.

## What was knowable, and when

- Shanghai Electric Power (SEP) first announced an intention to buy up to 66.4% of K-Electric on 27 Mar 2018 (`deals_history.csv`), withdrew in Dec 2018, and refreshed the intention repeatedly: the chain ran to a withdrawal on 22 Apr 2024 (deals_history) and a termination in September 2025 (searches, asserted).
- 28 Jun 2022, 14:43: "Intimation for extension in timeline for public announcement of offer by Shanghai Electric" (doc 189090). No price, no offer date.
- 11 Aug 2022: CEO re-appointed, new chairman. 26 Sep 2022: withdrawal of the intention to acquire 66.4% (doc 192791). 28 Sep: a fresh intention filed. 23 Sep: a power-station outage notice.
- Of 84 takeover records in `deals_history.csv`, 28 were withdrawn and 27 completed.

## How the move unfolded

Rs2.47 on 8 Jun. A volume build from 21 Jun: Rs2.67 (5.8m shares), 23 Jun Rs2.85 (+7.1% on 28m), 24 Jun 36.7m shares (6.5 times normal), 27 Jun Rs3.09 (+8% on 32m), 28 Jun Rs3.13, the day of the extension filing; the volume had started three sessions earlier. Then a fade to Rs2.84 by 2 Aug. A second burst: 15 Aug +6.3% on 58.6m, and on 17 Aug +13.0% to Rs3.73 on 209m shares, Rs780m, the peak; the next day minus 7% on 58m, Rs3.47. After that Rs3.35 to 3.53 until 29 Aug, minus 5.4% on 30 Aug (38m shares), Rs2.85 on 23 Sep, Rs2.76 on 4 Nov. No filing explains either burst (cause: unverified).

Recognition points: 27 to 28 Jun (volume, first leg; 1.19x ahead to the 17 Aug close), and 17 Aug itself (nothing ahead).

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

The first reasonable entry was the 27 or 28 Jun volume days at Rs3.09 to 3.13. There was a reasonable exit: the 18 Aug close at Rs3.47 after a 209m-share blow-off day, 11% above the entry; the best the move paid was 19% to the peak close. A 20% trailing stop sold on 21 Sep at Rs2.90 (0.93x, Rs93,000 on Rs100,000); a 30% stop on 28 Nov at 0.83x. A 12 Aug entry (Rs3.15) did the same: 0.92x and 0.83x. Holding to 16 Oct 2026 gave 1.95x of the 28 Jun price, but with a minimum of 0.50x on 21 Jun 2023, a trough a Rs100,000 holder would have felt as a 50% loss. Joining late did not pay.

## The trap

The label and the chain. A "takeover" filing without an offer price, repeated for years, is not a catalyst; the library tagged this run as a deal on the strength of one extension notice, and every attempt to read it as one is an episode of one, not a series (lesson 16: count episodes, not filings). A spike on 209m shares looks like demand and is the day to sell. Failure twins: PIOC (the real deal, a top at the offer), EPCL 2026 (situation 11) and the TPLT filing, where "interest" named no buyer.

## Questions that would have caught it

C3 (who decides and when: a Chinese buyer awaiting national clearances, no date), C2 and C6 (base rate: withdrawn almost as often as completed; no price, so no spread), C10 (one episode counted as many), D3 (a long causal chain, each link weak), A1 (a 13.75% to 15% hiking cycle), C9.

## The if-then rule

If a takeover filing is an extension or a renewed intention with no offer price, ignore it as a reason to buy; trade the volume if you must, as a three-week trade with a 10% stop, and sell the first minus 7% day after a spike above ten times normal volume.
