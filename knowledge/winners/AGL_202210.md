# Winner: Agritech, October 2022 to August 2025 (15.2x)

Written 2026-10-18. Sources: `data/winners_library.csv`, `data/prices_raw/AGL.csv` (no corporate action: raw equals adjusted), `data/announcements.parquet`, `data/macro/`. News (web search, 18 Oct 2026, snippets): Profit on the Maple Leaf and FFC bids (Nov 2024). The library labels it "other" because the takeover titles are not in the "material information" class; the deal flag did not fire.

## Dates and regime

Policy rate 15% (start), 22% in mid-2023, 11% at the peak. KMI All Share +194%. Agritech is not in the KMI All Share lists (then or now). It is a urea producer coming out of a restructuring: the scheme of arrangement completed on 4 Aug 2022, with preference shares held by banks.

## What was knowable, and when

- 4 Aug 2022: scheme of arrangement "completion date". Pre-start price Rs5.54.
- 11 Aug 2023 and 21 Dec 2023: "unusual movement in price" queries from the exchange. 25 Jul 2023: a +20% day.
- 29 Mar 2024: "price sensitive information: acquisition of shares by Maple Leaf Cement Factory"; 2 Apr: Alfalah Bank gave notice to convert preference shares into ordinary shares (again on 10 Mar 2025).
- 25 Jul 2024: Maple Leaf's public announcement of intention to acquire shares and control; 26 Jul: takeover-regulation disclosure.
- 11 Oct 2024: NBP sold its entire stake. 17 Oct: Maple Leaf acquired 27.7%. 25 Oct: FFC offered Rs38.84 for 151.05m shares (35.6%); 6 to 11 Nov: Maple Leaf's competing bid at Rs39.00 for 160.76m shares (37.9%); FFC raised to Rs39.05 and extended acceptance to 4 Jan 2025 (Profit).
- 19 Nov and 10 Dec 2024: material information on the offer revisions.

## How the move unfolded

Nine dead months: 0.76x to 0.88x of the start to June 2023. The spike days started in July 2023 (+20% on 25 Jul), with the first 252-day high on 7 Sep 2023 (quoted Rs7.2, 1.32x) and 2x on 24 Nov (Rs11.3). 3x on 12 Jan 2024, then a jump to 4.8x in March (Rs23.5 on 25 Mar, 5x on 1 Apr at Rs28.3) as Maple Leaf's purchases were disclosed. A 33% pullback to 11 Jun 2024, then 7x on 11 Sep (Rs38.8), 10x on 27 Jan 2025 (Rs57.4), a 43% crash into 9 May 2025, and the peak close of Rs84.32 on 25 Aug 2025, more than twice the Rs39 offers. 55 upper locks in all (19 in 2023, 15 in 2024, 12 in 2025). What held the price above a Rs39 control offer for ten months is unverified: a squeeze on a small free float is the obvious candidate.

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

Closing prices, 0.5% round trip, before tax.

- 8 Sep 2023 (first 252-day high, Rs7.14, 1.29x): 11.8x ahead. A 20% leash sold at -6% on 27 Oct; a 30% leash on 27 May 2024 at Rs19.69, +176%, Rs100,000 becoming about Rs274,000; a 40% leash on 8 May 2025 at Rs43.2, +505%, about Rs602,000, after a 41% drawdown.
- 27 Nov 2023 (2x, Rs12.2): +61% (30% leash).
- A buyer who took Rs39 as a ceiling and sold into the offer would have taken about +440% from Sep 2023 and missed the last 2x. The offers were not the top.
- Rs100,000 was 4% of a day's turnover at the start but under 0.2% from 2024, so size was never the issue; the 55 limit-up days were.

## The trap

Treating the offer price as the cap. The tender at Rs39.05 covered 35.6% of the shares; the free float's price was set by the uncovered rest. And the opposite trap: buying at Rs60 to Rs84 in 2025, 1.5x to 2.2x a control offer, with the control already decided. Failure twin: EPCL in 2026 (situation 11), where the offer was narrow and the price drifted back toward it.

## Questions that would have caught it

C6 (what fraction of my holding does the offer buy, and what is the stub worth), C3 (who gains and when: two named bidders with dates), C7 (volume with locks), B9 (float and exit), C9.

## The if-then rule this case suggests

If a strategic buyer is disclosing purchases under the takeover rules in a restructured company, buy on the first 252-day high and hold on a 30 to 40% leash; once a public offer is set, treat the offer price as the sell point for the part the offer covers and keep the rest on the leash, but do not buy above the highest offer.
