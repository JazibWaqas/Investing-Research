# Winner ASL, from March 2026: the sponsor's three blocks and the crowd that followed

Written 2026-10-18. Companion to `knowledge/situations/08_2026_asl_sponsor_buying.md`, which has the filings' detail; this file reads the same case as a winner. Sources: `data/winners_library.csv` (run 19 Mar to 15 Jun 2026, 1.51x, type shock rebound; the 9 Sep extension is mine), price and volume panels, `data/announcements.parquet`, PSX documents 273850, 275493, 277816, 280799, 281323 as cited in situation 08 (not re-opened). Prices are raw closes.

## Dates and regime

Low close Rs9.00 on 19 Mar 2026, peak Rs17.83 on 9 Sep (1.98x, 114 sessions), Rs15.09 on 16 Oct (minus 15.4% from the peak). War regime; policy rate 10.5%, hiked to 11.5% on 27 Apr; KMI All Share +13% over the span. Steel had no sector move: ISL and MUGHAL ended 31% and 37% below January highs. Median traded value Rs25m (tier H).

## What was knowable, and when

- By March: AHC had put Rs6.9bn of equity into the company in nine months (doc 275493, filed 28 Apr).
- 7 Apr purchase of 39.28m shares at Rs9.78 (to 15.67%), filed 9 Apr at 10:24 (doc 273850). 21 May: 27.0m at Rs12.27 (18.44%), filed 25 May. 3 to 4 Aug: 26.8m at Rs13.53 to 13.78 (21.19%), filed 5 Aug. 19 Aug: FY26 results with preference-dividend arrears from Jul 2022 declared (doc 281323).
- Exchange notices on the preference shares (ASLPS) on 5 Jun, 10 Aug and 2 Sep; text not read.

## How the move unfolded

The price fell from Rs14.01 (28 Jan) to Rs9.00 with the market. On 8 Apr it jumped 10.2% to Rs10.82 on 7.3m shares (4 times normal), a day before the letter was public. 9 Apr Rs10.97, 17 Apr Rs12.37, then a plateau at Rs11.85 to 12.3 for six weeks. The sponsor's own blocks are visible in the volume: 31m shares (22.7 times normal) on 21 May at Rs12.25 and 17m on each of 3 and 4 Aug (12 times). Between the blocks, 10 Jun brought +7.7% on 18m shares with no filing. The final leg ran after the last block (no later purchase filing found), with larger volume than any block: Rs14.92 on 17 Aug, Rs15.29 on 19 Aug (27.2m shares, the results day), then +10% on 3 Sep (Rs16.08, 19m), 35m shares on 4 Sep, Rs17.33 on 8 Sep, Rs17.83 on 9 Sep (28.6m). Then Rs15.09. The first close above the prior 252-day high was 3 Sep at Rs16.08, with only 1.11x left.

Recognition points: 9 Apr at Rs10.97 (71% of the log gain ahead; 1.63x left), 26 May after the second block (1.46x), 5 Aug after the third (1.31x), 13 Aug when volume turned (1.28x), 3 Sep (1.11x).

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

First reasonable entry: the 9 Apr close, 12% above the sponsor's price. Staying in: the worst pullback after entry was 15%, so even a 20% trailing stop was never hit. Reasonable exit: a close 20% below the high, or news that the sponsor stopped buying and sold, or a result that cancels the improvement.

Rs100,000, price-return, before costs:

| entry | price | to the 9 Sep peak | on 16 Oct |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 Apr | 10.97 | Rs163,000 | Rs138,000 |
| 29 May | 12.33 | Rs145,000 | Rs122,000 |

Joining late still paid, but by less each time; after 3 Sep it did not. A 20% stop would still be open at 16 Oct on all four entries, which means the exit was the 15% fall, not a stop.

## The trap

Three, as in situation 08: the price moved 10% before the disclosure; the preference arrears sit ahead of common holders; and a steel-sector view would have mislabelled it. A fourth: the library calls this run a shock rebound, a label that would have made a reader sit out a stock-specific trade. The last leg (Aug to Sep) had volume but no new sponsor information.

## Questions that would have caught it

C8 (is the sponsor buying, filed and public: yes, three times), B6 (how the sponsor treats minorities: Rs6.9bn injected, but preference arrears), C4 (confirmation versus extension: the 8 Apr gap was extension, the August volume was confirmation), C9 (the exit), B9 (Rs25m a day, fine).

## The if-then rule

If a controlling sponsor discloses repeated open-market purchases after an equity injection, enter on the first disclosure close with a 20% trailing stop, add only after a new disclosure, and take profits when the sponsor stops buying and volume exceeds anything the sponsor bought: the crowd leg after the last block is the one to sell into, not to chase.
