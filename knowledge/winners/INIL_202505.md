# Winner: International Industries (INIL), May to September 2025

Written 2026-10-18. Type: shock rebound and sector wave. Sources: `data/winners_library.csv`, `data/prices_raw/INIL.csv` and `data/panel_close_adj.parquet`, `data/announcements.parquet`, `data/fundamentals/quarterly_eps.csv` (unverified against filings), PSX document 258106 (the year to June 2025, read from the text layer), `knowledge/situations/06`. ## Dates and regime

Library start and low 8 May 2025 (raw Rs120.16, adjusted 113.37). Policy rate 12%, cut to 11% on 5 May; CPI 0.3%; USD/PKR 281. KMI All Share 44,072 on 8 May and 63,888 on 15 Sep (1.45x; INIL 2.03x). The library lists six steel peers moving plus 47% over the run, and Mughal Iron and Steel had the same start date and a 2.0x run to 22 Dec 2025. The case-06 shock explains the start.

## What was knowable, and when

1. 25 Apr 2025, 14:14: Q3 FY25 EPS 4.12 against 2.14 (EPS file, +93%). The stock closed at Rs149.20 (minus 1.1%) on thin volume (141k shares) and then fell 8% in the next three sessions.
2. 7 May: material information on incorporating an "INIL Europe Limited". Strikes began; INIL minus 6.7% on 7 May and minus 9.9% on 8 May (Rs120.16). 9 and 12 May: plus 9.4% and plus 10.0% (Rs144.58).
3. 3 Jun: plus 8.4% to Rs178.30. 3 Jul: plus 9.1%. 19 Aug: a rebrand announcement, plus 2.9% and the first 252-day high.
4. 22 Aug, 08:01: the year to June 2025, unconsolidated EPS Rs8.37 against 11.17 (minus 25%); consolidated profit Rs1.58bn against Rs3.83bn (minus 59%); final dividend Rs4 (doc 258106). Trailing P/E was already 27.6 on 19 Aug (EPS file). The stock moved less than 1%.
5. 15 Sep: the peak, Rs243.41 (plus 6.0% that day). 29 Oct: Q1 FY26 EPS 2.56 against minus 0.97.

## How the move unfolded

There was no first sign in the filing: it came in a falling stock. The shock took Rs149 to Rs120 in a fortnight; the rebound recovered Rs144 in three sessions, and the stock then advanced in steps: Rs178 on 3 Jun (1.48x the low), Rs194 on 3 Jul, a pause through July, Rs199 on 19 Aug (first 252-day high, with only 28% of the log gain left), and a last jump of 22% in four weeks to Rs243.41 on 15 Sep. The run had one limit-up close (12 May) and ordinary volume (Rs60m a day at best, in August). After the peak it fell 25% by 27 Nov (Rs178.82) and 32% by October 2026.

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

- Bought the 25 Apr filing (Rs140.58 adjusted at the next open): the worst close was 19% under cost on 8 May, 20 sessions in, which is the "failed within 20 sessions" case. A 20% trail survived 8 May by luck (the close was 19.97% under the highest close since entry), then exited on 31 Oct 2025 at plus 29%; 25% exited on 28 Nov at plus 22%; 30% on 17 Feb 2026 at plus 13%. Rs50,000 to about Rs64,700 (20%) or Rs61,100 (25%).
- Bought the rebound confirmation on 12 May (Rs139.64): never below minus 3%, plus 30% (20% trail), plus 23% (25%). The better entry, and available on the same price.
- Bought the 3 Jun jump: plus 7% (20%), minus 6% (30%). Bought the 19 Aug first 252-day high (Rs187.76): minus 3% (20%), minus 9% (25%), minus 16% (30%).
- What kept you in: the trend of higher lows since 12 May; the exit was a 20 to 25% fall from the highest close (31 Oct to 28 Nov). The first reasonable entry was the low; the highest-confidence entry (a 252-day high) came with 22% left and lost.

## The trap

Three. The label said earnings were accelerating; the year said they fell 59%, and the August results (the one filing the stock could have reacted to) changed nothing. The run was market-driven: the shock low, the rate cut and a steel wave. The 252-day high was the late entry. And the quarter after the peak (EPS 2.56 against a loss) did nothing to stop a 25% fall. Sister case: Mughal Iron and Steel (same dates, 2.0x, then a 41% fall within 60 sessions of its peak, per the library); the failure twin here is the 19 Aug entry.

## Which questions would have caught it

A9 and A11 (the shock, how it is priced), B1 (one quarter against four: FY25 down 59%), B11 (P/E 27.6 on trailing), C4 (extended: 28% left at the first high), C9 (exit), D5 (separating the market from the stock: index 1.45x, INIL 2.03x).

## The if-then rule

If a quarterly jump files in the same week as a market shock and the stock falls with the market, treat the filing as a reason to put the name on the rebound list, not a reason to buy it: buy after the stock closes back above its pre-shock level (12 May here), size for a 20% drawdown, and exit on a 20 to 25% fall from the highest close. Do not pay for a 252-day high when the share of the gain already made is above about 70%. One case.
