## Dates and regime

Start 28 Dec 2022, three weeks before the KSE-100 closed at 38,342 (17 Jan 2023). The same regime as UBL: rates 17% to 22% through Jun 2023, staff-level agreement 29 or 30 Jun, KSE-100 +5.9% on 3 Jul, first cut 10 Jun 2024, 12% by Jan 2025. A 100bp hike on 27 Apr 2026 to 11.5% followed the Hormuz shock of 28 Feb. MEBL is in KMI All Share and KMI30.

Quarterly EPS in the file: 9.12 (Dec 2022, filed 16 Feb 2023), 8.63, 9.66 (filed 10 Aug), then 14.43 on 19 Oct 2023, a 49% jump in one quarter, then 13.9 to 15.0 through 2024, 11.9 to 13.7 through 2025, 14.32 for Jun 2026. ## What a late but attentive reader could have done

The first reasonable entry was 21 Jul 2023, a close above the 252-day high on value three to five times the June level. No stop between 20% and 40% fired by 16 Oct 2026: Rs100,000 became Rs649,000. The 20% stop was 0.3 points from firing on 19 Dec 2024. Entry on 8 Dec 2023 (2.0x) returned 4.1x, with a 15% drawdown on the way; on 9 Dec 2024 (3.2x), 2.55x. Joining late paid at every point tested. Taking profit when the multiple had doubled on falling earnings (about Dec 2025, 6.2x of start) would have sold a stock that went on to 9.0x. Liquidity was never a constraint at Rs50,000 to Rs200,000.

## The trap

The first six months: down 11% by June 2023 on a stock with 3.9x earnings that looked cheaper every week. The second trap is paying a 10x multiple for the last leg: the 2026 gain came while EPS was 14% below its 2024 level and rates were turning up. The failure twin is BIPL, 2.6x, a smaller Islamic bank in the same list,.

## Questions that would have caught it

A4, A1, B1 (earnings power: EPS tripled before the price did), B3 (return on capital), B11 (what is priced in: 4x then 10x), B13, A6, C4, C9.

If the best-run KMI bank closes above its 252-day high within three weeks of the IMF trigger, buy and hold on a 25% closing stop (it did not fire in 800 sessions); trim only when the price-to-earnings ratio has more than doubled while quarterly EPS falls.
