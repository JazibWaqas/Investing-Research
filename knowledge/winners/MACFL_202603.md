## How the move unfolded

The first sign was volume, a day before the filing. On 29 Apr the stock opened at the limit (Rs26.69, 1.3m shares); on 30 Apr it opened Rs27.00 and closed at the limit, Rs29.36, on 14.1m shares. From 4 to 13 May it closed at the limit in eight sessions in a row, Rs32.30 to Rs62.94 then fell 10% on 14 May on 7.0m shares from an intraday high of Rs68 and 7.7% the day after. A plateau of Rs52 to Rs70 lasted until 12 Aug, with the first 252-day high on 15 Jun (Rs66.13, plus 10%). The second acceleration was 13 to 19 Aug (locks to Rs88.42, a Rs97.26 intraday high and Rs92.56 close on 19 Aug), and then four lower closes at the limit and a fall of 39% to Rs56.54 on 28 Aug. After the 2 Sep results it rose to Rs69.92 (6 Oct), then locked up six times from 7 to 14 Oct to Rs123.87. On 15 Oct it opened at Rs132.10, closed at Rs111.48 on 12.9m shares, and fell to Rs100.33 on 16 Oct. Log gain still ahead: 83% at the filing close, 37% after the May locks, 16% at 19 Aug.

## The trap

Volume before the news is the sign of an informed or leaking buyer and also what operator names do. Four PSX letters in 11 months, each answered with a denial, belong to the second reading, and there was no filing explaining the August or October legs. The Rs0.45 levy credit flatters EPS. The late entries (after 13 May, at the 252-day high) lost. Failure twins: the August blow-off (Rs92.56 to Rs56.54 in seven sessions) and now Rs123.87 to Rs100.33 in two. ## The if-then rule

If a small liquid-enough name trades 10x its volume on the day of a board meeting and then files a quarter with operating profit up more than double, buy the first session in which it trades below its limit price, size for a 25% fall, and sell on a 25% trail, not at a target. Do not add after the locks. One case; the PSX-letter history says treat it as a trade.
