# Winner: Pakistan State Oil, May 2023 to October 2025

Written 2026-10-18. Type: the largest OMC in a market recovery, with a cause the repo cannot name. Sources: `data/prices_raw/PSO.csv`, `data/winners_library.csv`, `data/announcements.parquet`, `data/fundamentals/quarterly_eps.csv`, Profit and ProPakistani snippets via search (asserted). PSX docs 244915 and 245037 were downloaded but their pages are images that did not render; I did not read them. Multiples are total return (price only 4.51x at the peak). PSO is a KMI member (derived lists, from 13 Jan 2020).

## Dates and regime

- 23 May 2023: policy rate 21% (22% from 26 Jun), CPI 38.0%, 3-month bill 22%, USD/PKR 287, KSE 41,099. IMF Stand-By staff agreement 29 Jun, Board 12 Jul (case 04).
- 14 Oct 2025: rate 11%, CPI 6.2%, bill 11%, USD/PKR 281, KSE 165,476, Brent $63.

## What was knowable, and when

1. The repo's quarterly EPS for PSO is lumpy: -5.20 (Jun 2023 quarter), 51.10 (Sep 2023), -25.09 (Dec 2023), 9.45, 3.58, 6.07 (Sep 2024), 13.42 (Dec 2024, filed 13 Feb 2025), 52.59 (Mar 2026), -29.64 (Jun 2026, filed 25 Sep 2026). A trailing P/E is meaningless here.
2. 25 Oct 2024 (13:02): Q1 FY25 results; the price rose 9.6% that day to a new 252-high, the first since 8 Dec 2023.
3. 24 Dec 2024 (09:55): PSO's reply to the exchange on unusual movement in its price (doc 244915); 26 Dec: a Material Information (doc 245037). Contents unread.
4. ProPakistani (13 Feb 2025, via search): half-year FY25 profit Rs11.2bn, roughly triple the year before. Receivables were Rs467bn at 31 Dec 2024, Rs340bn of it from SNGPL (search, asserted). No dated filing explains the December run. Cause: unverified.

## How the move unfolded

The first sign was the IMF rally: 1.27x on 31 Jul 2023, then a 20% fall to 1.02x by 31 Aug while the market held. The stock cleared 1.45x on 15 Nov 2023 (a first 252-high, 119 sessions in, 75% of the gain ahead) on volume of Rs658m a day, and made 2.01x on 8 Dec. Then a 35% fall to 1.31x on 16 Feb 2024, and eight months of 1.5x to 1.64x while the KMI All Share rose about 25%. It recovered the high on 25 Oct 2024 (2.08x).

The run itself was seven weeks: +9.2% on 27 Nov (2.65x), +9.7% on 12 Dec (3.15x), +10% on 23 Dec (4.04x), 4.12x on 1 Jan 2025, with a median value of Rs2.3bn a day in December. Then a 30% fall to 2.88x on 8 May 2025 (-9.9% that day, the India conflict, case 06), recovery by 22 Sep, 4.51x on 14 Oct 2025 and a high of 4.76x on 20 Jan 2026. A 35% fall to 31 Mar 2026 (the Hormuz closure, -10% on 2 Mar). 3.48x on 16 Oct 2026. Over the library window the other OMCs rose 2.7x (WAFI), 2.3x (HTL) and 2.1x (BPL).

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

Ahead of each entry (log share): 1.5x on 16 Nov 2023, 72%; 2x on 8 Dec, 54%; 3x on 12 Dec 2024, 24%; 4x on 23 Dec 2024, 7%.

- First reasonable entry: 16 Nov 2023 (1.53x), on the breakout and volume, or 25 Oct 2024 (2.08x), the re-breakout after eight months.
- To stay in: three pullbacks of 35%, 30% and 35% in 27 months. Only a 40% trail, or none, survived them.
- Reasonable exit: no price rule earned its keep. Selling on the 24 Dec 2024 notice (4.00x of start) beat every trail for a 25 Oct 2024 entry (1.92x of entry against 1.47x on a 25% trail and 1.67x held).
- Rs100,000 from 16 Nov 2023: Rs98,000 on a 25% trail (30 Jan 2024), Rs90,000 on 30%, Rs228,000 on a 40% trail or held to 16 Oct 2026. From 8 Dec 2023 (2.01x): Rs75,000, Rs68,000, Rs173,000. From 25 Oct 2024 (2.08x): Rs147,000 (25% trail), Rs167,000 (40% or held), Rs192,000 (sell on the notice). From 12 Dec 2024 (3.15x): Rs97,000 and Rs111,000. From 3 Jul 2023 (1.10x): Rs136,000 on 25%, Rs315,000 held. Exits chosen after the fact; n = 1; costs not deducted.
- Late entry still paid when it was a re-breakout after a long pause (25 Oct 2024); it did not pay inside the last leg.

## The trap

The 35% shake-out at 2.0x in December 2023 to February 2024: a reader who entered on the first 2x, as the investor might have, was stopped at 0.68x to 0.75x on any normal trail and missed the next 2.2x. Second, EPS that jumps between loss and profit gives no anchor for value. Third, a December spike on a name with Rs467bn of receivables and no stated cause. The failure twin is HTL, which made 2.3x and fell 38% afterwards.

## Questions that would have caught it

- B8: whether the cash is real; receivables and circular debt.
- C7: a spike with no filing; a company reply of "no information".
- C9: a stop wide enough for a 35% dip, or a size small enough to hold.
- A4, A8: the IMF and oil, the tide.

## The if-then rule

If a state-linked heavyweight with lumpy earnings re-breaks its high after a long pause, join with a 40% trail or a position small enough to sit through a 35% dip; after a doubling in about two months, treat an exchange notice on unusual movement as the sell signal. Do not enter on the first 2x.
