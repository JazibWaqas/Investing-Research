# Winner LUCK, from January 2023: the buy-back that rode the cement wave

Written 2026-10-18. Sources: `data/winners_library.csv`, price panels (split-adjusted, dividends excluded), `data/announcements.parquet`, PSX documents 192479, 204863, 207573 (read) and 221965 (scanned, not read), `data/macro/`. Prices before the April 2025 5-for-1 split are quoted in old rupees.

## Dates and regime

Library start 17 Jan 2023 (close Rs391.73), peak 22 Dec 2025 (6.6x, 724 sessions), 16 Oct 2026 at 5.1x. Policy rate 16% at the start, hiked to 22% by 26 Jun 2023, first cut on 10 Jun 2024, 10.5% by Dec 2025. IMF Board approval: 12 Jul 2023. KMI All Share rose 257% over the same dates. Maximum drawdown inside the run 19.8%, then 33% after the peak.

## What was knowable, and when

- 21 Sep 2022: public announcement of a buy-back of up to 10m shares (3.1%), window 29 Sep 2022 to 27 Mar 2023 (doc 192479).
- 11 Oct 2022 to 17 Mar 2023: daily purchase filings. I summed the annexure (doc 204863, 71 rows): exactly 10,000,000 shares bought at a weighted Rs435.64, from Rs512 in October down to Rs421 in March. The price fell about 24% while the company bought.
- 2 May 2023, 09:15: "Material Information - 2nd Buy-Back of Shares" (doc 207573): up to 23.8m shares (7.59%), spot price, 2 Jun to 20 Nov 2023, from distributable profits, with the stated aim of raising break-up value and EPS. EOGM approved 24 May.
- 16 Jun to 20 Nov 2023: daily filings. Actual shares bought: unverified (final report doc 221965 is a scanned image).

## How the move unfolded

The first buy-back ran five months into a falling market and did nothing visible. The low close was 17 Jan 2023 (Rs391.73); the price never closed more than 2% below it. It chopped at Rs403 to 445 into early May. The turn came after the 2 May announcement: Rs458 on 3 May on 11.9 times normal volume, Rs537 at the first 252-day high on 30 May (89 sessions after the start, with 83% of the log gain still ahead). It paused at Rs550 to 600 from July to September 2023, then accelerated in November (volume 4 to 15 times normal on nine days) to Rs787 by 30 Nov. It then drifted sideways for a year (Rs740 to 960 to mid-2024, Rs1,132 by 23 Dec), ran to Rs1,716 on 15 Apr 2025 before the split, and peaked at Rs512.7 post-split on 22 Dec 2025. Thirteen cement peers moved 2.9x to 9.7x (median 4.6x) over the same window (DGKC 5.8x, FCCL 5.3x, ACPL 5.1x, PIOC 8.2x, KOHC 4.6x); LUCK's 6.5x was inside that spread. Reasonable recognition points: 2 May 2023 (announced buy-back, rising tape; 5.9x of that price still ahead at the peak) and 30 May 2023 (first 252-high; 4.8x ahead, 83% of the log gain).

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

First reasonable entry: the first 252-high, 30 May 2023 at Rs537. Staying in: a 20% trailing stop on closes was never hit until 23 Feb 2026, but the worst pullback inside the run was 19.8%, so it escaped by a whisker; 25 to 30% is the honest setting. Exit: the 20% trailing stop fired on 23 Feb 2026 at Rs408 (80% of the peak multiple); the 30% stop on 9 Mar 2026 at about 67%.

## The trap

The label. A reader who concludes "management knows it is cheap" was right and learned nothing: DGKC, FCCL and PIOC did as well or better with no buy-back series, and the first buy-back lost 24% while running. The library tags the run sponsor or buy-back; the cause that explains it is the 2023 to 2025 cement and market wave. Failure twins: KOHC (stopped out of 4.5x by a 20% stop), MLCF (acquirer topping at the target's offer).

## Questions that would have caught it

A1 and A4 (rate at its peak, IMF Board ten weeks after the second buy-back), A6 (cheap against its own range), C1 and C2 (type: buy-back, no measured base rate), C4 (the 252-high was confirmation), C9 (stop width).

## The if-then rule

If a liquid, KMI cement or growth name is buying back stock at a policy-rate peak with the bill above the policy rate, treat the buy-back as a cheap-tide signal, not an edge: hold the best liquid name of the sector from the first 252-high with a 25 to 30% trailing stop and do not rely on the buy-back to defend the price.
