# Winner: International Steels, October 2016 to February 2017

Written 2026-10-18. Type: a short policy wave on one dated trade decision. Limit: prices start on 17 Oct 2016, so the true start is not visible and "start" is the first day of data; the weakest of the eight on that count. Sources: `data/prices_raw/ISL.csv`, `data/announcements.parquet`, Profit (19 Jan 2017, fetched), Business Recorder snippets via search (asserted; the pages returned 403). ISL is in the 5 Jun 2026 KMI list.

## Dates and regime

- 17 Oct 2016: policy rate 5.75%, CPI 4.2%, 3-month bill 5.9%, USD/PKR 104.6, Brent $49. KSE 41,283, KMI All Share 19,397 on 24 Oct.
- 9 Feb 2017: peak. Rate unchanged, Brent $54, KSE 49,908, KMI 23,737 (+22% since the first data day, against ISL +153%).

## What was knowable, and when

1. 19 Jan 2017 (Profit, fetched): the National Tariff Commission set anti-dumping duties of 13.17% to 19.04% on cold-rolled coil from China and Ukraine for five years, retroactive; the case was filed in July 2015. ISL and Aisha Steel were named beneficiaries. International Industries (INIL) holds 56.33% of ISL.
2. A duty on galvanised coil from China, from 8 Feb 2017 (search, asserted). ISL and Aisha raised prices by Rs2,000 a tonne from 14 Feb (Business Recorder via search, asserted).
3. Filings: Q1 results 18 Oct 2016; H1 results 24 Jan 2017 (14:49); director share sales on 13 and 14 Feb 2017, the first two sessions after the peak.
4. Business Recorder (asserted): the stock had nearly quadrupled in a year from Rs36 to Rs130.77 and hit Rs165 in February and again in May. The repo has no EPS for 2016 or 2017.

## How the move unfolded

Raw closes: 65.36 on 17 Oct, 56.61 on 27 Oct (-13%), 81.63 on 18 Nov, 97.32 on 4 Jan, 99.64 on 16 Jan, 121.36 on 26 Jan, 152.26 on 7 Feb, peak 165.40 on 9 Feb. The 19 Jan duty news fell between 99.64 and 121.36: the price moved on it (1.80x on 23 Jan) rather than before it, but it had already made 1.64x. The 24 Jan results day was a 5% lower-limit close (the 58% tax rate dragged earnings, per Business Recorder, asserted); the price then rose 48% to the peak in twelve sessions. After 9 Feb: 142.37 on 17 Feb, 137.76 on 1 Mar, a second top at the same Rs165 in May (2.47x at May end, Rs723m a day), then a 31% fall to 13 Jul.

Steel peers, 17 Oct to 9 Feb: INIL 1.98x, ASL 1.93x, DSL 1.64x, KSBP 1.40x, BCL 1.24x, MUGHAL 1.21x. Cement names, best close through 2017 from the first data day: LUCK 1.45x, DGKC 1.35x, FCCL 1.40x, CHCC 1.55x, PIOC 1.52x. Only the steel names with a protected product moved much.

## What a late but attentive reader could have done

Ahead of each entry (log share): 1.25x on 24 Nov 2016, 75%; 1.5x on 6 Jan, 56%; 2x on 1 Feb, 22%.

- First reasonable entry: 24 Nov 2016 at 1.26x (28 sessions in), a new high on rising volume (Rs153m monthly median), before the duty was known. The next honest entry was 19 Jan 2017 at 1.64x, the day of the duty.
- Staying through pullbacks: the run had only a 13% dip until February, so a 25% trail was enough.
- Exit: a 25% trail from the high (18 Apr 2017), or a 30% trail (3 May), or selling when two directors sold within a day or two of a 2.5x peak (14 Feb 2017).
- Rs100,000 from 24 Nov 2016 (1.26x): Rs149,000 (25% trail, 18 Apr), Rs140,000 (30%, 3 May), Rs190,000 (sell 14 Feb on the director sales), Rs104,000 held to 16 Oct 2026. From 6 Jan (1.51x): Rs124,000 on the 25% trail, Rs158,000 on the director rule. From 19 Jan (1.64x): Rs114,000 and Rs146,000. From 1 Feb (2.06x): Rs91,000 on the trail, Rs116,000 on the director rule. The exits were chosen after the fact; n = 1; costs not deducted.
- Where joining late stopped paying: from the day the duty news was in the price (about 1.7x), a trail exit lost money or earned under 15%.

## The trap

Buying the headline. The duty was dated and public on 19 Jan, but the stock had already made 64%; the steel names without a protected product (MUGHAL, KSBP, BCL) lagged. A second trap was the May double top at the same price, which looked like a new run and was followed by a 31% fall. The failure twin is MUGHAL: 1.21x to the peak and 0.82x today.

## Questions that would have caught it

- C3: who decides and when (the NTC, a dated ruling) and who gains (the named producers).
- C4 and B11: the price had moved before the decision; after it, the multiple was the risk.
- C9: write the exit before the headline.
- B2: which products are protected.

## The if-then rule

If a sector is moving on a dated trade or tariff ruling, buy only the named beneficiary, only before or at the ruling, and sell when insiders sell into the first peak; once the ruling is in the price, the next leg is short (this one, 82 sessions) and a trail exit gives most of it back.
