## 1. Where the market is, and what that decides

The State Bank raised its policy rate (the interest rate it charges banks, which sets the cost of money for everyone) to 11.5% in April. The six-month Treasury bill (a government IOU; its yield is what the market thinks rates will be) pays 12.4%, above the policy rate, so the market expects another hike. Inflation is 10.3% and flat. Oil is up about 50% in three months because the Strait of Hormuz is still disrupted. The KSE100 (the index of the 100 largest companies) closed at 165,152 on 20 Oct, below its January peak, with few stocks above their 50-day average price. The next rate decision is 26 October; 61% of brokers expect a hike (Topline survey, 17 Oct).

The library of every big move since 2016 says two things about a market like this. In the two previous hike cycles only about 4% of stocks rose at all; the money was made in a handful of names with their own story. And every run of 5x or more began at a regime turn, when a hike cycle ends and a cut cycle starts, usually within three months of a market low. We are not at one: the checklist scores one signal present, one absent, two unknown until about 10 November. So the plan has three parts: a core bought every month regardless, a reserve that waits for the turn, and at most one opportunity at a time, sized small.

## 2. The core: buy every month, hold through falls

Businesses, not trades. You buy them because the business grows, and you hold them through a 40% fall if the business is still growing. Each month's deposit is split between them; the one furthest below its target share gets the most.

**MEBL, Meezan Bank, Rs545. Target share of the core: 35% (more if the open slot goes to it). Next order: after the 22 Oct result, Rs12,500, about 23 shares, limit about Rs555, only if quarterly EPS is Rs11 or more.**
The story: the largest Islamic bank, growing deposits 20 to 25% a year by opening a hundred-plus branches a year, earning 34% on its equity (return on equity: profit divided by the shareholders' money in the business; 34% is exceptional). The best record of the twenty businesses scored, 16 of 20.
Why now: a bank earns the gap between what it pays depositors and what it charges borrowers; Meezan's deposits pay nothing, so a rate hike widens that gap immediately. It is the one core name that does well in exactly the regime we are in. The price is the problem: 3.3 times book value (what the shareholders' money is worth on paper), the highest in the sector, so the gain has to come from growth, not a re-rating (the market paying a higher multiple for the same profit).
What it could do on 23 shares in 12 months: bad year (a cut cycle starts, banks lag), Rs425, down Rs2,800; normal, Rs600, up Rs1,250 plus Rs580 of dividends after tax; good, Rs680, up Rs3,100. Frozen for the 22 Oct result: EPS Rs12.50 to 15 (65%), dividend Rs8 or more (60%).
What kills it: the first rate cut of a cycle. Islamic banks lagged the market by about 48 points in the six months after both previous first cuts (two episodes only). That is the signal to cut MEBL to a smaller position, over several deposits rather than in one sale, and move the money to the cyclicals the turn favours.

**MARI, Mari Energies, Rs628. Target share: 30%, but only Rs5,000 now (about 8 shares); the rest at or below Rs540.**
The story: the second-largest gas producer, new fields coming on (Spinwam from April, Shams from June, Lundali from September, Ghazij allocations building), oil at about $105. Profit last quarter was up 97% and the share has gone nowhere for three months. Scored 14 of 20; the weak points are a cut payout and money going to ventures abroad.
What the full card found (knowledge/companies/MARI.md): about 72% of sales are oil-linked by formula, but the formula caps the benefit above $110 oil, and gas prices reset half-yearly on past oil prices, so FY27 earnings barely move with Brent: about Rs64 a share at $75 and Rs67 at $125; oil mostly hits FY28 (Rs60 to Rs73). That makes it a volume story at a fair price, not an oil bet. The card's values: bear Rs468, base Rs627, bull Rs819 (25/50/25), expected return about +4% at today's price; full position only at or below Rs540.
What it could do on 8 shares: bad year (Iran deal, oil to $75, curtailments), Rs468, down Rs1,300; normal, Rs627, flat plus Rs170 of dividends; good (volumes plus a higher multiple), Rs819, up Rs1,500.
What kills it: curtailments (each 50 MMscfd of linked gas is about Rs10 of annual EPS), the Rs86bn of trade debts (Rs62bn overdue, with a provisioning exemption that expires 31 Dec 2026), or the state taking more per unit.

## 3. The reserve: money that waits for the turn

Twenty percent of every deposit is held back in the brokerage account as cash, because the library says the turn is where the 5x runs begin and the playbook says the first weeks of a turn are recognisable: oil calm, few stocks still making new lows, the rupee flat, reserves rising, then more than half of stocks above their 200-day average and about a dozen names at one-year highs. When the checklist scores three of four, a third of the reserve goes into the first liquid KMI names to make new one-year highs after the low (in past turns these differed by cycle; cement was the common thread), a third at four weeks, a third at eight. At Rs50,000 a month that is Rs10,000 a month. Release rule, so it never becomes dead cash: if the checklist has not scored 3 by 19 Oct 2027, half goes into the core and the reserve share drops to 10% until the checklist scores 2 (PLAYBOOK.md section A, with the evidence). The case against holding a reserve at all is in gpt/EXAMINER_NOTES.md section 1.1.

## 4. The opportunity: one at a time, small, with the exit written first

At most 5% of the whole book lost if the stop hits (a stop is a price at which you sell without discussion); exits frozen at entry and never widened; nothing with no visible cause.

**Now: nothing qualifies.** The refiners are the only names near one-year highs and they are late: PRL is over 4 times its low (of 18 comparable runs only 2 went further). The ATRL conditional entry did not trigger (it closed Rs1,190.74 on 20 Oct, below the Rs1,200 trigger).

**Triggers; each becomes an order only on the stated observation:**
- SAZEW (Haval assembler, 4 times earnings, a plant three times current sales): buy if quarterly EPS prints at or above Rs60 and the State Bank does not hike on the 26th. Stop 30% below entry. The first name to own when the turn comes.
- SYS at or below Rs100, or on the first close above its 50-day average after its quarterly result, whichever comes first.
- A results jump (quarterly profit up 40%+ with cash flow to match, not one-off income) in a liquid KMI name where the price closes up 10% on the filing: join within three sessions, stop 25%. The daily scan tags these.
- Any name at a one-year high with a visible cause and a stage below 2 times its low. The scan lists them.

**Exclusions, by evidence:** stocks rising on heavy volume with no filing and no sector move lose money for a small account; thin names under Rs5m a day of trading; anything already 4 times its low; buying a results filing before seeing the price react.

## 5. What the book most likely does

Over the next 12 months with no turn, the core tracks the market with a growth tilt, roughly −15% in a bad year to +25% in a good one, plus about 4% in dividends after tax. With a turn in the first half of 2027, the reserve and the cyclical names could add 30 to 60% from the low. A 10 to 15% monthly return does not exist in any year of the data except for one or two names a year that no rule or judgment caught in advance.
