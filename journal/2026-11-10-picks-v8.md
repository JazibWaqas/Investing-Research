## 1. What happened in the ten days

- **Oil collapsed.** Brent went from about $116 on 30 October to about $92 today, a fall of about 20%, the biggest weekly drop since 2020. Qatar carried an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to Washington, and the US said further strikes were not needed "at this point". The deal is not signed and the US naval blockade is still on, so this can reverse. But the thing I said would change everything has happened.
- **Inflation came in at 9.6%** for October (published 2 November), under 10% for the first time since the oil shock. With the policy rate at 12% that is a real rate of about +2.4, which means the State Bank now has room to stop hiking and, if oil stays down, to start cutting.
- **The market moved.** The KSE100 went from 166,409 to about 177,500, up 6.7%, and crossed its 200-day average (about 171,200) on 4 November. Of 293 KMI names with a year of history, 43% are now above their 200-day average (24% ten days ago), 49% above their 50-day (14%), and only 4% are at 20-day lows (19%). About nine liquid names are at one-year highs; FCCL is one of them.
- **Cement led:** DGKC +18%, MLCF +17%, FCCL +16%, KOHC +14%, LUCK +13%. Then ILP +7%, MEBL +6%, SAZEW +6%, MARI +4%. Refiners fell further (NRL -18%, PRL -15%, ATRL -14%): they were the oil-price trade, and it reversed.
- **Nothing new in the filings** for the names in play except a SAZEW clarification (2 November): its distributor, not the company, built a three-wheeler plant in the Philippines; no equity from SAZEW.

**The scorecard.** Thirty-one calls scored, 22 held, Brier 0.180 (0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip). The seven that came due since 30 October all held by outcome, but three of them I had under even odds (FCCL, FFC and OGDC all closed higher five sessions after their results; I had each at 0.45 to 0.47), so the odds were wrong even where the outcome went my way. The turn checklist did what I said it would (it did not reach 3 of 4 on the 23 October low: oil was up 35% over 13 weeks and 19% of names were at 20-day lows). The market turned anyway, on an oil reversal the checklist is not built for. What caught it was the override written in the last letter: "accelerate if oil falls 15% or more within a few days". That is now the record: the checklist is for balance-of-payments lows, the oil override is for shock reversals.

## 2. What I think now

**This is the shock-reversal pattern, and it is early.** In the data, every fall of 5% or more on a geopolitical shock recovered: 9 of 9 regained the pre-shock level, with a median gain of 28% at 60 sessions from the low (tests/noise_map_shock_rebound.md). The index is 9% off its 23 October low, so about a third of the typical move has happened. The pre-shock high was about 189,000 (6 July); that is 6.5% above today.

**What has changed about the odds.** On 30 October the downside was "oil keeps rising, another hike, the index falls 15%". Today oil has fallen 20%, inflation is under 10%, and the December decision is more likely a hold or a cut than a hike. The bad case still exists (the Hormuz deal collapses and oil goes back to $110 to $115), and I put it at about 30%. But the money you hold back now is no longer protecting you against the most likely path; it is costing you the most likely path.

**What has not changed.** The businesses. MEBL, ILP, MARI and LUCK are the same companies at prices 4 to 13% higher. That is the cost of not buying on 2 November, about Rs2,500 on the Rs45,000 plan. It is a small price. Do not let it become a reason to wait for the old prices; in the two fast turns in the data (2020, 2023), buying in week 1 beat buying in week 8 by 16 to 17 points.

**Is it a daily-trading market now? Still no.** The forty short-horizon rules I tested over ten years all lost to costs (knowledge/lessons.md 25). The one short-term pattern with evidence is the one you are in: buy the shock reversal and hold it for two to six months. Section 4 gives you one swing trade with a written exit, if you want one.
