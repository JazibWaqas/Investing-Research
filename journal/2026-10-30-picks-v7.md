## 1. What happened in the two weeks you did not watch

- **The State Bank raised its policy rate by 0.5 points to 12% on 26 October**, nine votes to one, because oil is high and it wants to stop that feeding into prices. The policy rate is the interest rate the central bank sets for the whole system; everything else is priced off it. I had "no change" at even odds, so that call was wrong. The next decision is 14 December.
- **Oil went the wrong way.** Brent (the world benchmark price for crude) is about $116, up 12% in October alone, because of attacks on tankers around the Strait of Hormuz. Pakistan imports its energy, so high oil means a higher import bill, more inflation and higher rates. This is the single thing holding the market down.
- **The rest of the economy is fine.** Reserves at the State Bank are $21.5bn and rising, the rupee is flat at about 277, the current account is near balance and the IMF programme is on track. This matters: the two previous hike cycles in the data (2018 to 2019 and 2021 to 2023) were balance-of-payments crises, with the rupee collapsing. This one is an oil shock on top of a repaired economy. That is a materially better starting point.
- **Results season was strong and the market did not care.** MEBL beat (quarterly profit per share Rs14.36, dividend Rs8) and the share fell 3%. LUCK grew its own cement profit 17% and the share fell 2%. MARI's profit rose 39%. FFC, OGDC, PSO and ILP all beat. Prices that do not rise on good news are the signature of a market where the interest rate, not the businesses, is setting values.
- **The refiners rolled over.** NRL is down 17% in two weeks, PRL down 9%, MACFL down 50%. In the last letters I said these were "late" (several times their lows) and not to chase them. That was right.

**My scorecard, honestly.** Twenty-four calls have now been scored: 15 held, Brier score 0.164 (a Brier score measures how good the odds were; 0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip). The misses were the rate decision and profit forecasts that were too low: FFC, FCCL and MEBL all beat the top of my bands. Lesson for me: in this results season companies beat, so my ranges should be wider and higher. The full record is in ledger/scorecard.md.

## 2. What I think of the market right now

**It is a market with no leadership, under a rate hike, with strong earnings underneath.** Of 116 liquid KMI stocks, only 24% are above their 200-day average price (a line that roughly says whether a stock is in an uptrend), only 14% are above their 50-day average, and not one is at a one-year high. The KSE100 is at 166,409: 12% below its January peak, 14% above its March low, and just under its own 200-day average (about 171,000). The median stock fell 3% over the last two weeks.

**Is it a daily-trading market? No.** Nothing is running, the one wave that was running (refiners) has ended, and my own ten-year study of short-term rules found none that beat costs. Buying good businesses and leaving them is the right posture for this market, which is what you asked for.

**What the history says about markets like this.** In the two previous hike cycles only about 4% of stocks rose and the index fell 20 to 37%. So the honest base rate is that stocks do not go up while rates are rising. Against that: this time the macro is sound, valuations are already low (the index trades at about 8 times earnings, which means an earnings yield of 12.7%, about the same as a Treasury bill at 12.65%, which you cannot buy anyway), and the thing holding the market down, oil, is a single event. If a US-Iran deal reopens Hormuz, oil could fall 15 to 25% in days, the State Bank's reason to hike vanishes, and this market would reprice fast. Every geopolitical fall of 5% or more in the data recovered within three months (9 of 9), and every run of 5x or more in ten years began when a hike cycle turned to cuts. Nobody can time the deal. What you can do is be meaningfully invested before it, in names that do not hurt you much if it is late, and have money ready to add when it comes.

**Where we are on the turn checklist** (the four signals that separated real market bottoms from false ones in the last ten years): rupee flat, yes; reserves rising, yes; oil calm, no (up 12% in a month); the "drain" (fewer than 10% of stocks making new lows), no (15 to 19%). Two of four. That is the same reading as the false bottom in September. Not a turn yet.
