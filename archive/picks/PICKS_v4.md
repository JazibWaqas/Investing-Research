## 1. Where the market is, and what that decides

The State Bank raised its policy rate (the interest rate it charges banks, which sets the cost of money for everyone) to 11.5% in April. The six-month Treasury bill (a government IOU paying a fixed return; its yield is what the market thinks rates will be) pays 12.4%, above the policy rate, which means the market expects another hike. Inflation is 10.3% and flat. Oil is up about 50% in three months because the Strait of Hormuz is still disrupted. The KSE100 (the main index, a basket of the 100 largest companies) is 13% below its January peak, and only 16% of liquid stocks are above their 50-day average price. The next rate decision is 26 October; 61% of brokers expect a hike.

The library of every big move since 2016 says two things about a market like this. First, in the two previous hike cycles only about 4% of stocks rose at all; the money was made in a handful of names with their own story (this year: the refiners, after a policy approval and an oil shock), not in the market. Second, every run of 5x or more in ten years began at a regime turn, the moment a hike cycle ends and a cut cycle starts, usually within three months of a market low, and we are not at one: the playbook's checklist scores one present, one absent, two unknown until about 10 November. So the plan has three parts: a core that is bought every month regardless, a reserve that waits for the turn, and at most one opportunity at a time, sized small.

## 2. The core: buy every month, hold through falls

These are businesses, not trades. You buy them because the business grows, and you hold them through a 40% fall if the business is still growing. Each month's deposit is split between them; the one furthest below its target share gets the most.

**LUCK, Lucky Cement, Rs401. Target share of the core: 35%. Monday: Rs12,500, about 31 shares.**
The story: the cement leader that has become a conglomerate, with a car plant (KIA), a power plant (Lucky Electric) and a chemicals company (LCI), run by a family that keeps building. It scored 15 of 20 on the business-quality record: it delivered what it promised and earned good returns on its big investments. Last quarter's profit was up 29%.
Why now: it trades at 6.8 times earnings (the share price divided by one year of profit per share; lower is cheaper) and 21% below its high, because cement is out of favour while rates are high. Cement and cars are the most rate-sensitive sectors, which is why LUCK led the market in June 2024 when the first cut came, and why it belongs in the core before the next turn, not after.
What it could do in 12 months, on 31 shares: a bad year (rates stay high, cement demand soft), Rs310, down Rs2,800; a normal year, Rs460, up Rs1,800 plus about Rs120 of dividends; a turn year (first cut in 2027), Rs600, up Rs6,200. The library's four turns say the cement leaders doubled or more in the year after each one.
What kills it: a privatisation cheque above about Rs35bn for LUCK's share (two thirds of FY26 operating cash flow of Rs54.8bn; the GEPCO central case is about Rs12.5bn on an even four-way split, with a win probability of about 15%, see knowledge/companies/LUCK_gepco.md), or cement demand falling. Watch the quarterly result in late October and any board approval of a DISCO equity commitment.

**MEBL, Meezan Bank, Rs546. Target share: 35%. Monday: Rs12,500, about 23 shares.**
The story: the largest Islamic bank, growing deposits 20 to 25% a year by opening a hundred-plus branches a year, earning 34% on its equity (return on equity: profit divided by the shareholders' money in the business; 34% is exceptional). The best record of the twenty businesses scored, 16 of 20.
Why now: a bank earns the gap between what it pays depositors and what it charges borrowers; Meezan's deposits pay nothing, so a rate hike widens that gap immediately. It is the one core name that does well in exactly the regime we are in. The price is the problem: 3.3 times book value (what the shareholders' money is worth on paper), the highest in the sector, so the gain has to come from growth, not from a re-rating (the market paying a higher multiple for the same profit).
What it could do on 23 shares: bad year (a cut cycle starts, banks lag), Rs425, down Rs2,800; normal, Rs600, up Rs1,250 plus Rs580 of dividends after tax; good, Rs680, up Rs3,100. Quarterly result Wednesday 22 October; we expect earnings per share (profit divided by number of shares) of Rs12.50 to 15 and an Rs8 dividend.
What kills it: the first rate cut of a cycle. Islamic banks lagged the market by about 48 points in the six months after both previous first cuts. That is the signal to cut MEBL to a small position and move the money to LUCK and the cyclicals.

**MARI, Mari Energies, Rs631. Target share: 30%, but only Rs5,000 now (about 8 shares); the rest at or below Rs540.**
The story: the second-largest gas producer, new fields coming on (Spinwam from April, Shams from June, Lundali from September, Ghazij allocations building), oil at $105. Profit last quarter was up 97% and the share has gone nowhere for three months. Scored 14 of 20; the weak point is a cut payout and money going to ventures abroad.
What the full card found (knowledge/companies/MARI.md, 19 Oct): about 72% of its sales are oil-linked by formula, but the formula caps the benefit above $110 oil, and gas prices are set half-yearly on the previous months' oil, so FY27 earnings barely move with Brent now: about Rs64 a share at $75 and Rs67 at $125; the oil price mostly hits FY28 (Rs60 to Rs73). That makes it less of an oil bet than I said and more of a volume story at a fair price. The card's values: bear Rs468, base Rs627, bull Rs819 (25/50/25), expected return about +4% at Rs631, buy at or below Rs540.
What it could do on 8 shares now: bad year (Iran deal, oil to $75, curtailments), Rs468, down Rs1,300; normal, Rs627, flat plus Rs170 of dividends; good (volumes plus a higher multiple), Rs819, up Rs1,500. The same arithmetic on the full position only works from Rs540.
What kills it: curtailments (each 50 MMscfd of linked gas is about Rs10 of annual EPS), the Rs86bn of trade debts (Rs62bn overdue, with a provisioning exemption that expires 31 Dec 2026), or the state taking more per unit. Probability of an Iran deal taking Brent to $75 by mid-2027: about 30%, a judgment; it matters for FY28, not FY27.

Not in the core, and why: SYS is the only export compounder but still 23 times earnings with an unresolved fear about AI pricing; it goes on the watchlist for a lower entry. FABL, OGDC, FFC and HUBC are sound but have no engine to double earnings; they were version 2's core and are kept as paper positions for scoring.

## 3. The reserve: money that waits for the turn

Twenty percent of every deposit is held back in the brokerage account as cash. Not because cash is safe, but because the library says the turn is where the 5x runs begin, and the playbook says the first weeks of a turn are recognisable: oil calm, few stocks still making new lows, the rupee flat, reserves rising, then more than half of stocks above their 200-day average and a dozen names at one-year highs. When the checklist scores three of four, a third of the reserve goes in at once into the leaders the playbook names (cement, autos, the largest names), a third at four weeks, a third at eight. Until then it accumulates. At Rs50,000 a month that is Rs10,000 a month; by the time the turn comes there may be Rs50,000 to Rs100,000 waiting, and that is the money that can make a year. Release rule, so it never becomes dead cash: if the checklist has not scored 3 within 12 months (by 19 Oct 2027), half of it goes into the core and the reserve share drops to 10% until the checklist scores 2; cash never exceeds 25% of the book (PLAYBOOK.md section A, with the evidence: past waits from a false bottom to the real low were 10 and 11 months).
